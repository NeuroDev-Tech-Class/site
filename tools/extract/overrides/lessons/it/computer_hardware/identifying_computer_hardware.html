You've read and watched how memory, storage, power and cooling work. Now find each of them inside a real computer and read what's written on it. You'll open one of the tech class desktops with your coach.

Safety first. Shut the computer down completely and unplug every cable before you open it. Touch a metal part of the case before touching anything inside, to discharge static electricity. Never open the power supply itself: it can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged.

Find and read each part

  1. Memory (RAM). Find the RAM sticks next to the CPU. Count them and read the label on one: note its capacity (for example 8 GB), its type (DDR4 or DDR5) and its speed if it's shown. Are the sticks in the slots your motherboard recommends for dual channel?
  2. Storage. Find every storage drive. Is each one an M.2 SSD on the motherboard, a 2.5-inch SATA SSD, or a 3.5-inch hard drive? Note its capacity from the label.
  3. Power supply (PSU). Find the power supply, usually at the top or bottom of the case. Read its sticker without opening it: note its wattage and any efficiency rating (such as 80 Plus Bronze or Gold).
  4. Cooling. Find how the CPU is cooled: an air cooler with a heatsink and fan, or a liquid cooler with a radiator. Count the case fans and work out which way the air flows through the case.
  5. Take a photo of the inside of the case if you can, then close it up, plug everything back in and check the computer still starts.
