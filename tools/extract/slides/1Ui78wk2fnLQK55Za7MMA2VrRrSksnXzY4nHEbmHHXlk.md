## What is Social Media?

- Social media is any online platform that lets users to create and share content, as well as interact with each other through comments and messages. They tend to form virtual communities based on shared interests and connections.
- Examples include YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, Snapchat, LinkedIn, TikTok, Pinterest, Reddit, and Discord.

## What is Social Media?

- In 2021, 70% of teenagers in the U.S. said they use social media multiple times a day, up from 34% in 2012 (Common Sense Media, 2021).
- Social media use has been linked to both positive and negative effects on mental health, social skills, and self-esteem.

## Access to Information

- Social media platforms offer real-time news updates and a space for sharing ideas and opinions, allowing users to learn from different perspectives and viewpoints.
- 48% of young adults in the U.S. get their news from social media (Common Sense Media, 2019).
- It can also be a tool for online learning and collaboration.

## Mental Health & Well-being

- Social comparison and “FOMO” (fear of missing out) can be detrimental to mental health
- 27% of teenagers feel social media negatively affects their self-esteem
- 43% of teenagers in the U.S. report experiencing cyberbullying on social media

## Mental Health & Well-being

- Social media addiction is linked to negative mental health outcomes (self-harm, suicidal behavior, poor life satisfaction)
- Media multitasking is linked to negative effects on cognitive control, academic performance, social skills

Source: ScienceDirect - Social media use and its impact on adolescent mental health

## Privacy & Security Concerns

- Social media platforms collect vast amounts of user data, raising privacy concerns and potential misuse of personal information.
- Users also need to be aware of cyber threats such as phishing and identity theft, emphasizing the need for strong security measures and vigilance in online interactions.

## Misinformation & Fake News

- Social media can allow false information and fake news to spread rapidly and influence public opinion.
- The ease of sharing content on social media platforms can contribute to the rapid dissemination of misinformation.

![Magnifying glass over a newspaper headline zooming in on the word "Fake"](/images/slides/social-networking-the-pros-and-cons/16.webp)

---

## Misinformation & Fake News

- Read past the headline: Check the content for more context and don’t assume that the headline provides the full picture.
- Check the author: Verify the credibility and expertise of the source.
- Evaluate other sources: Cross-reference the information with other reliable and reputable sources.
- Consult experts: Reach out to those who know about the subject.
- Check your biases: Be aware of your own biases and how they may influence your judgment of the information. Beware of echo chambers.
- Consider if it's satire: Make sure the content isn't intended as humor or satire, as these can sometimes be mistaken for genuine news.

## Decreased Productivity

- Exposure to blue light and constant mental stimulation can disrupt the body’s production of melatonin, making it harder for the brain to wind down and prepare for sleep.
- 45% of teenagers in the U.S. said they are online almost constantly (Pew Research Center, 2018).

## Finding Balance

- Turn off notifications: Disable non-essential notifications to reduce distractions and fight the urge to constantly check your phone.
- Set time limits: Use your device settings to allocate specific periods for social media use and avoid excessive screen time, especially before bedtime.
- Prioritize meaningful interactions: Focus on connecting with close friends and family and minimize exposure to negative or unproductive content.
- Cultivate offline hobbies and relationships: Participate in activities that don’t involve screens and foster face-to-face interactions to build stronger personal connections.
- Schedule a digital detox: Regularly set aside time for unplugging from social media and electronic devices, allowing for relaxation and reconnection with offline activities.
