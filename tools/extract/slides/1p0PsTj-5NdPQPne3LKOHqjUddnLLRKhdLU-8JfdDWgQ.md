## Prompt Engineering vs. Problem Formulation

---

## What is prompt engineering?

Prompt engineering is a set of strategies used to get better results from your generative AI prompts.

While AI models have made great strides recently, they’re still far from perfect. Many users have tried to develop strategies that give them better results.

## Prompt engineering tips

#1. Be clear and precise

The more vague your prompt is, the more vague the AI’s response will be. Be as direct and specific as possible. Remember that you’re not talking to a mind reader, or a human for that matter. AI models can’t always read between the lines as well as you might expect.

![Arrow stuck in the bullseye of a target](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/02.webp)

---

## Prompt engineering tips

#2. Iterate

To “iterate” means to repeat and improve a process over time.

Start with a basic prompt, see how it performs, look for errors, improve it, and repeat. Swap out words and sharpen your prompt until you get the results you want. Take note of how your changes impacted the result.

![Chalkboard diagram of a circular cycle with four colored arrows](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/03.webp)

---

## Prompt engineering tips

#3. Itemize

Break complex multi-step tasks into small manageable pieces. AI models do better with step-by-step instructions, much like people.

For example, instead of asking it to write you a children’s book, ask it to give you a rough outline, and then use that outline to have it write one page at a time.

![Isometric illustration of colorful blocks stacked like a city, broken into pieces](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/04.webp)

---

## Prompt engineering tips

#4. Provide examples

If you need the AI to answer you in a specific way, it can be helpful to give it an example of what a good answer looks like.

For example: “Make me an ‘80s New Wave music playlist. Here is an example of what your answer should look like: …” and show it how you’d like it to be formatted.

![Painting of a boy drawing a tree while a robot copies his example](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/05.webp)

---

## Prompt engineering tips

#5. Role prompting

Sometimes it helps to begin your conversation by giving the AI a role.

“You are a professional career coach.”

“You are an expert computer hardware technician.”

“You are a 5-star chef.”

![Cartoon robot with four arms holding a spatula, pencil, wrench, and book](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/06.webp)

---

## Prompt engineering tips

#6. Avoid negation statements

Sometimes, AI models get confused when asked not to do something. If I asked you to not think about elephants, you’d probably think about elephants.

Rather than asking the AI model NOT to do something, ask it to DO the thing you need it to do.

![Crossing guard holding up a stop sign and a raised hand](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/07.webp)

## Example

![AI-generated aerial view of a busy city street still full of cars, despite being asked to remove them](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/08.webp)

![AI-generated street scene with pedestrians and cyclists instead of cars](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/09.webp)

“draw a city without any cars”

“draw a city with only bikes and pedestrians”

Neither prompt produced perfect results, but the second prompt did much better. Telling DALL·E not to draw cars actually made it difficult for it to avoid drawing cars. If you’re having trouble getting the results you want, try rephrasing your negation statements into positive assertions.

## Prompt engineering tips

#7. Use delimiters

A delimiter is a sequence of characters that helps to visually separate different parts of text.

For example, you might use symbols like \`\`\`triple backticks\`\`\` or \<angle brackets> to help the AI distinguish between different parts of your prompt.

![Screenshot of a chat prompt using slashes to separate different customer reviews](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/10.webp)

## Limitations of prompt engineering

Here’s the thing: all of the tips and tricks we’ve discussed seem to work in certain scenarios, but they’re not bulletproof.

The field of generative AI is advancing and changing extremely rapidly. It’s very likely that AI models will very soon become so effective that most of these tips and tricks will have to be modified. Some might not work the same way at all.

More importantly, no amount of prompt engineering will help as long as the user isn’t able to communicate what they actually want.

---

## What is problem formulation?

Problem formulation is a powerful communication skill. It’s the ability to accurately and precisely define a problem. It’s about understanding the problem you’re trying to solve.

Sometimes the problem isn’t in the way you’re phrasing your question; it’s in not fully understanding the question in the first place.

![Pixel-art illustration of a glowing light bulb](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/11.webp)

---

## Steps to effective problem formulation

Identify the “problem”. Clearly define what you actually want generative AI to accomplish in this scenario, whether it’s answering a difficult question or helping you create something new.

Understand the context. Gather background information and learn about the constraints specific to your problem.

![Comic-style magnifying glass zooming in on the word "Context"](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/12.webp)

---

## Steps to effective problem formulation

Break Down the Problem. Decompose it into smaller, manageable parts. Sometimes it’s better to solve it one piece at a time. Maybe start with an outline of the problem and go from there.

Develop Hypotheses. Try to come up with possible solutions on your own, and then use these to guide your prompts.

![Ornate antique-style diagram of circles and spheres representing an astronomical model](/images/slides/prompt-engineering-vs-problem-formulation/13.webp)

---

## Integrate both approaches!

As you sharpen your problem-solving and communication skills, you will be more capable of using generative AI as a tool rather than a crutch.
