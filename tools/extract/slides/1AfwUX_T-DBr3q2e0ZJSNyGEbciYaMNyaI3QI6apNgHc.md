## What is AI?

AI has become such a buzzword in recent years that it can be hard to nail down its exact definition, which is actually pretty broad.

AI, or artificial intelligence, refers to the simulation of human intelligence processes by a computer algorithm. This definition encompasses all sorts of different systems.

## What is AI?

Some of these intelligence processes include learning (gaining information), reasoning (using rules to reach conclusions), and self-correction.

It does not have anything to do with sentience or self-awareness, but rather the ability of an algorithm to solve problems by adjusting its own parameters to improve performance. This is called machine learning.

![Three illustrated panels showing AI reading, processing, and generating ideas](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/02.webp)

## What is generative AI?

Generative AI refers to AI models that generate new data similar to the data they were trained on.

Unlike older AI models that mostly focused on classification or prediction, generative models aim to create new content, whether it's text, images, or other forms of data.

![Glass pyramid emitting a rainbow burst of small icons and symbols](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/03.webp)

---

## How does generative AI work?

Generative AI models are trained on huge amounts of data, learning the underlying patterns and relationships.

During the training process, the model adjusts its parameters to minimize the “loss function” (the difference between the generated outputs and the desired outputs). Once trained, the model can generate new data instances when given an input prompt.

![Abstract layered diagram of a data structure resembling a circuit board brain](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/04.webp)

## How does generative AI work?

Large Language Models (LLMs) like OpenAI’s ChatGPT and Google’s Gemini are some of the most popular generative AI models. These models are trained on gigantic sets of text from the internet and other sources. By learning the underlying patterns and structures of language, LLMs can generate human-like responses to text prompts.

![Glowing branching network of light connecting to pages of text](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/05.webp)

## How does generative AI work?

Text-to-image models like DALL·E and Midjourney work in a similar way, except they transform text input into images rather than text. They are trained on image-caption pairs and learn to associate words with millions of different visual properties.

![Glowing branching network of light connecting to a grid of colorful image tiles](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/06.webp)

## How does generative AI work?

Generative AI models are usually neural networks, which are made of layers of interconnected nodes called “neurons”. They behave like a simplified version of the neurons in your brain, in that they respond to input stimuli and transmit output signals.

Neural networks use this cascading effect to transform an input signal into an appropriate output.

## How a neural network is trained

### Data Gathering

The first step in training a neural network is to collect a large and diverse dataset that represents the “problem domain” (for example, images with captions). The data is preprocessed to clean it and transform it into a format suitable for training.

### Initialization

A suitable neural network architecture is chosen based on the nature of the problem. The model's initial parameters, such as weights and biases, are usually set randomly.

### Forward Pass

Input data is fed through the network, and predictions are made. At first, the model essentially has to guess randomly and is usually very wrong. The model’s output is compared to the expected “correct” output using a loss function.

### Backpropagation

The error signal is propagated backward through the network, and the model's parameters are adjusted to minimize the loss using optimization algorithms like gradient descent. Then we do another forward pass and repeat the process.

---

## How a neural network is trained

During the forward pass / backpropagation loop, the model is validated or evaluated on a separate dataset (data that was not used during training) to monitor and fine-tune performance. This also helps developers decide when to stop training the model.

If a model is trained for too long there is a risk of overfitting, which is when the model learns the training data “too well”. A model that is overfitted to training data will start to perform more poorly against testing data, because it has learned to spot random patterns (noise) that only exist in the training data. This makes it harder for the model to generalize.

## AI Misconception #1

“AI can only copy existing data.”

While generative AI can mimic training data, it can also create entirely novel ideas based on patterns it has learned from its training data. The AI model works within its own model of reality called a "latent space," which is a multi-dimensional compressed representation of everything it has learned during training.

## AI Misconception #2

“AI always tells the truth.”

AI models are far from perfect and often generate outputs that are false or nonsensical. They don’t "lie" in the human sense; lying implies intent to deceive, which AI lacks. Instead, outputs can be inaccurate due to limitations in the model or the training data. It has no capability of distinguishing between truth and lies.

![Robot being sworn in with one hand raised in a courtroom](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/11.webp)

---

## AI Misconception #3

“AI models are unbiased.”

AI systems will inherit and perpetuate any biases in the data used to train them. In other words, an AI model is as objective as the dataset used to train it; no more, no less. These biases can lead to unfair or discriminatory results.

![Robot depicted as Lady Justice, holding a scale and sword](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/12.webp)

---

## AI Misconception #4

“AI will replace us all!”

While AI has the potential to automate certain tasks and jobs, it’s not going to completely replace us any time soon. Some jobs will be replaced, some will become easier for us, and some new jobs will be created. This is a cycle that repeats whenever a game-changing technology emerges.

See: the Industrial Revolution, the Internet, etc.

![Robot dressed as a farmer in overalls and a straw hat, standing in a cornfield](/images/slides/intro-to-generative-ai/13.webp)

---

## AI Misconception #5

“AI will lead to superintelligent machines taking over the world.”

AI research has made significant strides, but even human-level general intelligence is still a far distant goal. Concerns about AI posing existential threats are speculative and highly sensationalized. That said, there are still plenty of ongoing discussions about safety and ethics.
