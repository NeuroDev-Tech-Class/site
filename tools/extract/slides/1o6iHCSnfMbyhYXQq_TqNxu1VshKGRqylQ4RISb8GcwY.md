## Internet and the World Wide Web

### Internet

![Simple globe icon representing the Internet](/images/slides/the-internet-the-world-wide-web/01.webp)

The Internet is a global network of interconnected computers and devices. Historically, the Internet was built on top of the existing telephone network. Today, the vast majority of Internet traffic is transmitted over fiber optic cables, which use pulses of light to transmit data over long distances.

### World Wide Web

![Icon of three overlapping browser windows representing the World Wide Web](/images/slides/the-internet-the-world-wide-web/02.webp)

The World Wide Web (WWW) is a system of linked web pages which are accessed through the Internet. Websites are hosted on web servers, and users must enter their Uniform Resource Locator (URL) to access them. URLs are made up of a protocol, domain name, and specific page or resource.

## A brief history of the Internet

The Internet wasn’t invented by a single person, but was a collaborative effort from many researchers and scientists over several decades. It was developed in the late 1960s by the US Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) to allow researchers to easily communicate with each other. In the ‘70s, standardized communication protocols allowed these networks to be interconnected globally, leading to the creation of the Internet.

![Black and white photo of researchers working with early computer equipment](/images/slides/the-internet-the-world-wide-web/03.webp)

---

## A brief history of the Internet

The World Wide Web (WWW) was invented by Sir Tim Berners-Lee in 1989. It was designed as a way to easily share information across the Internet via hyperlinks, and was first made available to the public in 1991. In the mid ‘90s, web browsers such as Netscape Navigator and Microsoft's Internet Explorer popularized the WWW, and search engines like Yahoo! and Google revolutionized the way people found information online.

![Tim Berners-Lee sitting beside an early computer displaying the first World Wide Web page](/images/slides/the-internet-the-world-wide-web/04.webp)

## “Why do I need to know this?”

The Internet has become an integral part of our daily lives, impacting the way we communicate, access information, work, and engage with others. We can better appreciate the efforts and innovations that have shaped the digital landscape by learning about its history. We can also keep up with new technological developments, security issues, and potential future developments. Understanding the Internet and the WWW empowers us to be more informed, responsible, and adaptable digital citizens.
