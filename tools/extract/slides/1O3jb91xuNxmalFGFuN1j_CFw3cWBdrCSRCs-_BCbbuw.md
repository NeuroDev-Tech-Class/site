## What is “ethics”?

Ethics is the philosophical study of morals. It asks the question, “how should we live?” In other words, what are the “shoulds” and “should nots” of society?

Ethics allows us to navigate the complexities of right and wrong, fair and unfair. It mirrors our collective conscience and acts like a compass for behavior in society.

## Ethics in the age of AI

The ongoing rise of generative AI has unleashed a number of new and complicated moral questions, both for developers and for users. And as the pace of AI improvement accelerates, these challenges will snowball. It’s important to answer these questions as we move forward.

## Core principles of AI ethics

#1. Transparency

AI systems should be clear and understandable by humans. Clarity allows for trust and accountability.

For example, an AI loan approval system that explains its decisions based on credit history and income, allowing applicants to understand and trust the process.

![Transparent glass head with a computer chip in place of a brain](/images/slides/ai-ethics/03.webp)

---

## Core principles of AI ethics

#2. Fairness

AI should not perpetuate or amplify biases, intentionally or not.

A real-world negative example: an AI recruitment tool used by Amazon was found to be biased against women. The system favored male candidates over female candidates for technical roles.

![Simple line drawing of a balance scale](/images/slides/ai-ethics/04.webp)

---

## Core principles of AI ethics

#3. Privacy

AI should respect and protect individuals' privacy.

For example, a smart assistant that listens to your voice (like Alexa or Siri) should not keep recordings of what you say or sell your data to advertisers without your explicit permission.

![Hands holding a tablet with a glowing digital shield and lock icon](/images/slides/ai-ethics/05.webp)

---

## Core principles of AI ethics

#4. Accountability

Developers and deployers of AI are accountable for its actions.

If an AI system is found to pose danger to humans in any way, the company that deployed it is responsible for correcting it immediately.

## How to use AI responsibly

### Learn how it works

![Close-up collage of interlocking brass and steel gears](/images/slides/ai-ethics/07.webp)

Understand the purpose and the limitations of the AI systems you use. Be aware of what data you’re sharing and configure your privacy settings to protect personal information.

### Use it as a tool

![Close-up photo of a hammer with a wooden handle](/images/slides/ai-ethics/08.webp)

Use AI to study for a test, not to cheat on your homework. The line between these two can seem blurry sometimes, but it’s helpful to ask yourself: “is this helping me learn the subject, or is it just letting me pass the class?”

### Avoid plagiarism

![Robot standing beside a student writing at a desk in a classroom](/images/slides/ai-ethics/09.webp)

Even though AI produces unique content, it might copy copyrighted writing or intellectual property. Be careful about copy-pasting text from ChatGPT. Instead, use it as a study buddy, and then write in your own words.

### Own your actions

![Cartoon boy pointing accusingly at a small robot after breaking a window with a baseball](/images/slides/ai-ethics/10.webp)

Much like AI developers are responsible for making sure it doesn’t cause harm, users are responsible for the way they use AI. Using AI to indirectly harm others is the same as doing it yourself. This includes cyberbullying and spreading disinformation.
