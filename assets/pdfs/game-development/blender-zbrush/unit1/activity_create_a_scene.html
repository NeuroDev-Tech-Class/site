Blender & ZBrush Mini

Activity: Create a 3D Scene

Put your modeling skills together to build a complete scene

Overview

In this activity, you'll use everything you've learned so far — navigation, transforms, Edit Mode, Extrude, Inset, Loop Cut, and materials — to create a complete 3D scene in Blender. This is your chance to be creative and show off your new skills!

Your scene should represent a small environment, like a desk setup, a room corner, a kitchen counter, or an outdoor picnic area. The theme is up to you.

Requirements

Your scene must include the following:

1. At Least 3 Different Objects

Model at least three distinct objects. They can be simple — we're not looking for movie-quality realism. Here are some ideas based on difficulty:

Easier Medium Challenging
Table Chair Lamp with shade
Bookshelf Mug / Cup Computer monitor
Box / Crate Book (open or closed) Desk with drawers
Step stool Plate Game controller
Bench Pencil / Pen Robot character
Tip: Start with the easiest objects and work your way up. It's better to have three well-made simple objects than one rushed complex one.

2. Use Extrude, Inset, and Loop Cut

At least 2 of your objects must demonstrate the use of:

  • Extrude (E) — to create legs, handles, raised or recessed features
  • Inset (I) — to add panel details, screen areas, or decorative elements
  • Loop Cut (Ctrl+R) — to add geometry for more detailed shapes

You don't need to use all three tools on every object, but across your scene, all three should be demonstrated on at least two objects.

3. Apply Materials

Each object in your scene must have a different material color. For example:

  • A brown table
  • A red mug
  • A blue book

You're welcome to use multiple materials on a single object (like a black screen on a silver monitor) for bonus points.

4. Arrange Into a Scene

Don't just leave objects scattered randomly. Arrange them into a believable scene. Place objects on top of each other, next to each other, or in logical positions. A mug on a table looks like a scene — a mug floating in empty space does not.

Use G (grab/move) to position objects. Use R (rotate) to angle them naturally. Use S (scale) to make sure objects are proportional to each other.

5. Render a Screenshot

When your scene is complete:

  1. Position the camera to frame your scene nicely (select the camera, then use G and R to place it, or press Numpad 0 to enter camera view and use Ctrl+Alt+Numpad 0 to snap the camera to your current view)
  2. Switch to Material Preview or Rendered shading mode so materials are visible
  3. Go to Render > Render Image (or press F12)
  4. In the render window, go to Image > Save As to save your render as a PNG file

Requirements Checklist

Before submitting, make sure you can check off every item:

✓ Requirement
☐ At least 3 different modeled objects in the scene
☐ Extrude (E) used on at least 2 objects
☐ Inset (I) used on at least 2 objects
☐ Loop Cut (Ctrl+R) used on at least 2 objects
☐ Each object has a different material color
☐ Objects are arranged into a logical, coherent scene
☐ Camera is positioned to frame the scene
☐ A rendered screenshot (PNG) is saved and submitted
☐ The .blend project file is saved and submitted

Submission

Submit the following files:

  1. Your .blend file (the Blender project)
  2. Your rendered screenshot (PNG image)
Have fun with it! This is a creative project. Add extra objects, experiment with different materials, try unusual color combinations. The more you practice, the faster you'll get. There's no wrong way to build a scene as long as you meet the requirements.
Save frequently! Press Ctrl+S after every major step. There's nothing worse than losing an hour of work because you forgot to save.
