Blender & ZBrush Mini

Final Project

Design, Sculpt, Print, and Finish a Showcase Piece

Overview

For your final project, you'll create a showcase piece that demonstrates everything you've learned in this course — from digital sculpting or modeling all the way through 3D printing and finishing. This is your chance to make something entirely your own: an original creation that shows off your skills, creativity, and personal style.

Requirements

1. Choose Your Tool

Design and create your piece using ZBrushCoreMini (sculpting) or Blender (polygon modeling) — student's choice. You may also combine both (sculpt in ZBrush, refine in Blender) if you'd like to demonstrate the roundtrip workflow.

2. Demonstrate Multiple Tools / Brushes

Your project must show that you can use a variety of tools, not just one or two. Specifically:

  • If sculpting in ZBrushCoreMini: Use at least 4 different brushes (e.g., Move, ClayBuildup, Standard, Pinch, Smooth). Your sculpt should show evidence of blockout (large forms), medium detail, and fine detail.
  • If modeling in Blender: Use at least 3 different techniques or modifiers (e.g., extrude, loop cuts, Subdivision Surface, Mirror, Bevel, Boolean). Your model should demonstrate clean topology.

3. Intentional Design — Sketch It First

Before you touch the computer, sketch your idea on paper. Your sketch doesn't need to be a masterpiece — it just needs to show that you planned your design ahead of time. Include:

  • A front view of your design.
  • A side or three-quarter view.
  • Notes about details, dimensions, or features you want to include.
  • Reference images you're drawing inspiration from (printed, sketched, or described).

Intentional design separates a thoughtful creation from random sculpting. Plan ahead, then execute.

4. Proper Export

Export your finished model correctly:

  • From ZBrushCoreMini: use the Printer Export icon to save as OBJ.
  • From Blender: export as STL with correct scale and "Selection Only" checked.
  • Verify the file opens correctly in your slicer before printing.

5. Successful 3D Print

Your project must be 3D printed. This means:

  • Load the file in your slicer and configure appropriate settings (layer height, infill, supports if needed).
  • Orient the model for best print quality.
  • Preview the sliced layers and verify everything looks correct.
  • Print the object. If the first attempt fails, troubleshoot (re-orient, add supports, adjust settings) and try again — problem-solving is part of the process!

6. Sanding and Painting

Apply what you learned about finishing:

  • Sand the print (at least two grits — coarse and fine).
  • Prime with spray primer.
  • Paint with at least two colors of acrylic paint.
  • Optional: Apply a clear coat for protection.

Project Ideas

Not sure what to make? Here are some ideas to get you started:

  • Pendant or keychain: A wearable sculpt with a loop for a chain or keyring. Great for gifts!
  • Figurine: A small character, creature, or animal. Could be original or fan art of a favorite character.
  • Custom game piece: A chess piece, tabletop RPG miniature, or board game token with your own design.
  • Decorative object: A small sculpture, desk ornament, or display piece.
  • Functional item: A custom phone stand, pencil holder, or small container.
  • Badge or emblem: A 3D logo, crest, or symbol with raised details.

Inspiration and References

Every great artist uses references. Gather inspiration before you begin:

  • Search for reference images of similar objects (animals, characters, shapes).
  • Look at existing 3D prints on sites like Thingiverse or MyMiniFactory for ideas.
  • Study real-world objects for proportions and details — hold them, turn them around, notice the shapes.
  • Save or print your references so you can look at them while working.

Evaluation Criteria

Category What We're Looking For
Creativity Is the design original and thoughtful? Does it show personal style? Was it planned with a sketch?
Technique Were multiple tools/brushes used effectively? Does the model show evidence of intentional sculpting or modeling decisions? Is the topology clean (Blender) or the sculpt well-formed (ZBrush)?
Finish Quality Was the print sanded, primed, and painted? Is the paint job clean with thin, even coats? Does the final piece look polished and complete?
