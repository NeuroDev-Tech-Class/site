Sculpting Techniques

A Detailed Guide to Every Brush in ZBrushCoreMini

The Sculpting Mindset

Sculpting in ZBrushCoreMini is very different from modeling in Blender. Instead of placing individual vertices and edges, you're painting on a surface — pushing clay outward, carving grooves inward, smoothing rough areas, and pulling forms into shape. Every brush has a specific behavior, and knowing when to use each one is the key to efficient sculpting.

Remember: for every brush, holding Alt reverses its effect (add becomes carve, inflate becomes deflate, etc.). And holding Shift at any time temporarily switches to the Smooth brush.

Brush-by-Brush Guide

Standard Brush

The Standard brush is your all-purpose tool. It adds soft, rounded buildup to the surface — imagine pressing your thumb gently into clay to raise a bump. The stroke creates a smooth dome shape wherever you paint.

  • Normal use: Adds material (raises the surface).
  • Alt + stroke: Carves into the surface (subtracts material).
  • Best for: General detail work, adding subtle bumps and dips, refining surfaces.
  • Tip: Use a medium draw size and moderate Z-Intensity (25-40) for controlled, gradual buildup.

ClayBuildup Brush

The ClayBuildup brush adds material in a rough, square-edged fashion — like pressing strips of flat clay onto the surface. Unlike the Standard brush's soft dome, ClayBuildup creates more defined, angular forms. It builds up more aggressively and is great for establishing large forms quickly.

  • Normal use: Adds thick, flat-topped buildup.
  • Alt + stroke: Removes material in flat planes.
  • Best for: Building up medium-scale forms — muscles, cheekbones, brow ridges, armor plates. This is a workhorse brush for the "rough-in" phase.
  • Tip: Use overlapping strokes to build forms gradually. The square-edged deposits blend together nicely when smoothed afterward.

Inflate Brush

The Inflate brush pushes every polygon outward along its own normal direction — like inflating a balloon. Instead of building in one direction, it puffs the surface outward evenly in all directions at the stroke point.

  • Normal use: Bulges the surface outward.
  • Alt + stroke: Deflates — pushes the surface inward, creating sunken areas.
  • Best for: Puffing out rounded areas like cheeks, lips, eyelids, or swollen/puffy effects. Also great for making organic shapes look more full and alive.
  • Tip: Use with low Z-Intensity (15-25) — Inflate can quickly distort your mesh if applied too heavily.

Pinch Brush

The Pinch brush pulls nearby vertices toward the center of the stroke, creating sharp creases, ridges, and tight edges. It's like pinching clay between your fingers to create a sharp ridge.

  • Normal use: Creates sharp creases and defined edges by pulling geometry together.
  • Alt + stroke: Spreads geometry apart, creating a shallow groove or softening a crease.
  • Best for: Defining sharp features — eyelids, nostrils, lip lines, wrinkles, armor edges, or any place where two forms meet at a defined edge.
  • Tip: Use after the Standard or ClayBuildup brush to sharpen up transitions between forms. A tiny bit of pinch goes a long way.

Move Brush

The Move brush grabs a section of the mesh and shifts it in the direction you drag. It doesn't add or remove material — it simply repositions the existing geometry. Think of grabbing a lump of clay and pushing it to the side.

  • Normal use: Shifts geometry in the direction of your stroke.
  • Best for: Blockout phase — establishing the major proportions and silhouette of your sculpt. Moving the nose forward, pushing the chin out, repositioning ears, adjusting the overall shape.
  • Tip: Use a large draw size for big, sweeping adjustments early on. The Move brush is your best friend during the first 20% of any sculpt — get the big shapes right before worrying about detail.

SnakeHook Brush

The SnakeHook brush pulls long, tentacle-like extensions out of the surface. Drag it outward and the mesh stretches dramatically, creating horns, tendrils, spikes, or flowing shapes.

  • Normal use: Pulls long extensions from the surface.
  • Best for: Creating horns, tentacles, flowing hair shapes, decorative swoops, or any elongated organic form.
  • ⚠️ Caution for 3D printing: SnakeHook can create very thin, delicate extensions that may be too fragile to print successfully. If you plan to 3D print your sculpt, keep SnakeHook extensions reasonably thick, or be prepared for them to break easily.
  • Tip: Use with Symmetry on to create matching pairs of horns, antennae, or wing-like shapes.

Slash3 Brush

The Slash3 brush carves stylized indent lines into the surface — like dragging a knife or chisel across clay. It creates clean, defined grooves with a consistent depth.

  • Normal use: Carves a defined groove into the surface.
  • Best for: Decorative lines, battle scars, panel lines on armor, stylized wrinkles, or any linear detail you want to engrave into the surface.
  • Tip: Use confident, single strokes rather than going over the same line multiple times. Long, smooth strokes produce the cleanest results.

hPolish Brush

The hPolish (Horizontal Polish) brush flattens areas of the mesh, creating planar surfaces. It pushes high points down and pulls low points up until the surface is level.

  • Normal use: Flattens the surface under the brush stroke.
  • Best for: Creating flat bases for printing, flattening the bottom of a figurine so it stands upright, polishing rough areas into clean planes, creating hard-surface looks (mechanical or architectural shapes).
  • Tip: Great for making a sculpt look more "designed" and intentional. After roughing in organic forms, hPolish certain surfaces to imply machined or manufactured areas (like flat armor plates).

Smooth Brush (Shift)

The Smooth brush averages out the surface, softening bumps, ridges, and rough areas. You don't need to select it from the brush menu — simply hold Shift while stroking to temporarily smooth at any time, regardless of which brush is currently active.

  • Normal use: Smooths and softens the surface.
  • Best for: Blending rough strokes together, eliminating unwanted bumps, creating smooth skin or polished surfaces, cleaning up after aggressive brush strokes.
  • Tip: Sculpting is a constant back-and-forth between adding/carving and smoothing. Build up a form, smooth it, add more detail, smooth again. This iterative process is how professional sculptors work.

Recommended Workflow Order

Here's a suggested order for tackling a sculpt, using the brushes above:

  1. Blockout (Move brush): Start with the Move brush at a large Draw Size. Push and pull the mesh into the rough overall shape. Get proportions and silhouette right. Spend time here — a strong blockout makes everything easier.
  2. Primary forms (ClayBuildup): Switch to ClayBuildup to add medium-scale volume — muscles, clothing folds, major surface features. Build up material where you need it, carve away where you don't. Smooth frequently.
  3. Secondary detail (Standard + Pinch): Reduce your Draw Size and use the Standard brush for smaller bumps, dents, and surface variation. Use Pinch to sharpen edges where forms meet — around eyes, along jaw lines, at armor seams.
  4. Fine detail (Slash3, Standard at small size): At the smallest brush sizes, add wrinkles, pores, scratches, decorative patterns, and other surface texture.
  5. Polish (hPolish + Smooth): Clean up any areas that need flattening. Do a final smooth pass over areas that should be sleek.

Key Takeaways

  • Alt reverses any brush (add ↔ carve, inflate ↔ deflate).
  • Shift smooths at any time, regardless of the active brush.
  • Start big, go small: Move → ClayBuildup → Standard/Pinch → Slash3/Fine detail → Polish.
  • Rotate your model constantly to check all angles.
  • Sculpting is iterative — build, smooth, refine, repeat.
