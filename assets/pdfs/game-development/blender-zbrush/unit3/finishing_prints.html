Finishing Prints

Sanding, Priming, Painting, and Caring for Your Printer

Why Finish a 3D Print?

A 3D print straight off the build plate shows visible layer lines — thin horizontal ridges across the entire surface. While the shape is correct, the surface doesn't look polished or professional. Post-processing is the collection of techniques used to transform a rough print into a smooth, painted, finished piece. This is where your creation truly comes to life.

Sanding

Sanding is the most effective way to remove layer lines and smooth the surface of a PLA print. It takes patience, but the results are dramatic.

How to Sand a 3D Print

  1. Start with coarse sandpaper (120-grit): This removes the most visible layer lines and any blobs or imperfections. Use firm, even strokes. Don't press too hard — let the sandpaper do the work. Sand in small circles or back-and-forth motions.
  2. Move to medium sandpaper (220-grit): This smooths out the scratches left by the coarse paper. The surface should start feeling noticeably smoother.
  3. Finish with fine sandpaper (400-600 grit): This creates a very smooth surface ready for priming. At this point, the layer lines should be nearly invisible.

Sanding Tips

  • Work in a ventilated area. Sanding PLA creates fine plastic dust. Open a window or work near a fan.
  • Wear a dust mask. You don't want to breathe in plastic particles. A simple disposable dust mask is sufficient.
  • Wet sanding produces the smoothest results. Dip the sandpaper in water and sand the surface while wet. This reduces dust, prevents clogging of the sandpaper, and produces a finer finish. Use wet sanding for the final pass with 400-600 grit paper.
  • Be careful on thin areas. Sanding removes material. If your print has thin walls or delicate details, sand gently to avoid breaking through.
  • Check your progress by wiping the surface with a damp cloth. Layer lines are easier to see on a clean surface.

Priming

Spray primer serves two purposes: it fills in any remaining tiny layer lines that sanding didn't completely remove, and it provides a uniform base color for painting.

How to Prime

  1. Make sure the print is clean and dry. Wipe off all sanding dust with a damp cloth and let it dry completely.
  2. Work in a well-ventilated area (outside or in a garage is ideal). Lay down newspaper or a drop cloth to protect your work surface.
  3. Hold the spray can 8-12 inches away from the print.
  4. Apply thin, even coats using smooth, sweeping motions. Don't try to cover everything in one thick coat — that causes drips and hides detail.
  5. Let each coat dry for 10-15 minutes before applying the next. Two to three thin coats are usually enough.
  6. Once the primer is fully dry, you can lightly sand with 600-grit sandpaper for an extra-smooth surface before painting.

Recommended primer: Gray or white spray primer from any hardware store works well on PLA. "Filler primer" is even better — it's formulated to fill small surface imperfections.

Painting

With a smooth, primed surface, your print is ready for color!

Painting Tips

  • Use acrylic paint. Acrylic craft paint (from any art supply store) adheres well to primed PLA, is water-based (easy cleanup), and comes in every color imaginable.
  • Thin your paint. Thick paint hides detail. Add a small amount of water to the paint until it flows smoothly off the brush without being watery.
  • Apply thin coats. Two or three thin coats always look better than one thick coat. Each thin coat dries quickly and builds up an even, smooth finish.
  • Let each coat dry fully before applying the next. Rushing causes smudges and uneven color. Acrylic paint typically dries in 15-30 minutes.
  • Use small brushes for details and larger, flat brushes for broad areas.
  • Dry brushing: For highlighting raised details, dip a brush in paint, wipe most of it off on a paper towel, then lightly drag the nearly-dry brush across the surface. Paint will catch only on the raised edges, creating a highlighted effect.
  • Washes: Thin paint heavily with water to create a wash. Apply it over painted surfaces — the thinned paint flows into crevices and recesses, adding depth and shadow. Wipe excess off the raised areas with a cloth.

Clear Coat for Protection

Once your paint job is complete and fully dry, apply a clear coat spray to protect the paint from chipping, scratching, and fingerprints. Clear coat comes in three finishes:

  • Matte: No shine — a flat, subtle look.
  • Satin: Slight sheen — a natural, balanced appearance.
  • Gloss: Shiny and reflective — great for polished or wet-looking surfaces.

Apply clear coat the same way as primer: thin coats from 8-12 inches away, with drying time between coats.

Cleaning the 3D Printer

Taking care of your printer keeps it working well and producing quality prints.

  • Clean the build plate after every print. Remove any stuck plastic, and wipe the surface with isopropyl alcohol to ensure good adhesion for the next print.
  • Run cleaning filament through the nozzle periodically. Cleaning filament is a special material designed to push out residue and buildup from inside the hot end. Heat the nozzle, feed in cleaning filament, and extrude until it comes out clean.
  • Check for debris on the rails and moving parts. Wipe them gently with a dry cloth. Small bits of plastic can accumulate and affect print quality.
  • Keep the nozzle clear. If you notice under-extrusion (thin, patchy layers), the nozzle may be partially clogged. Running cleaning filament or using a nozzle cleaning needle can help.

Safety Reminders

  • 🔥 Nozzle is HOT. Never touch the nozzle during or immediately after printing or cleaning.
  • Ventilate when sanding, priming, painting, and applying clear coat. Fumes and dust are not safe to breathe in enclosed spaces.
  • Wear a dust mask while sanding. A simple disposable mask is enough.
  • Wash your hands after handling spray primer, paint, and clear coat.
  • Use spray products outdoors or in a well-ventilated garage. Never spray primer, paint, or clear coat in a small, closed room.

Key Takeaways

  • Sand from coarse to fine (120 → 220 → 400-600 grit) to remove layer lines.
  • Prime with spray primer to fill remaining lines and prepare for paint.
  • Paint with thin coats of acrylic — patience produces the best finish.
  • Clear coat for long-lasting protection.
  • Clean your printer regularly for reliable results.
  • Safety first — ventilation, dust masks, and avoid hot surfaces.
