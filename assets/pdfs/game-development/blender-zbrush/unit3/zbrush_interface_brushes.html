ZBrush Interface & Brushes

Getting Around ZBrushCoreMini — Tools, Navigation, and Controls

Welcome to ZBrushCoreMini

ZBrushCoreMini is a free, simplified version of ZBrush — the industry-standard digital sculpting software used in movies, games, and product design. Unlike Blender's polygon-based modeling (working with vertices, edges, and faces), ZBrush is all about sculpting. You push, pull, carve, and smooth a digital ball of clay using brushes, just like sculpting with your hands — but with a stylus and tablet.

The interface looks very different from Blender, but don't worry — ZBrushCoreMini is intentionally stripped down to the essentials. Once you learn where the tools are, it's surprisingly intuitive.

Interface Overview — Tool Icons

ZBrushCoreMini's interface uses a clean row of icons along the top and sides. Here's what each one does:

Top Toolbar

  • 📂↓ Open Project (Folder-Down icon): Opens a previously saved ZBrushCoreMini project file. Use this to continue working on a project from a previous session.
  • 📂↑ Save Project (Folder-Up icon): Saves your current project. Save frequently! ZBrushCoreMini project files use the .ZPR format.
  • 🖌️ Brush Menu: Opens the brush selector, showing all available sculpting brushes. Click a brush to select it. We'll cover each brush in detail in the next lesson.
  • ➕ Create New Mesh: Starts a new sculpt from a fresh base shape (sphere, cube, etc.). This replaces your current mesh, so save first!
  • 🔄 Symmetry Toggle: Turns symmetry on or off. When symmetry is on (default), everything you sculpt on one side is automatically mirrored on the other side. This is perfect for faces, creatures, and any symmetrical design.

Brush Controls

  • Draw Size: Controls how large the brush is. A bigger draw size affects a wider area of the mesh. Drag the slider or type a number. Start large for broad forms, then decrease for fine detail.
  • Z Intensity: Controls how strong each brush stroke is — how much it pushes, pulls, or carves with each stroke. Higher intensity = more dramatic effect. Lower intensity = subtle changes. A value around 25-50 is a good starting range.

Mesh Resolution

  • Poly Size (Low / Med / High): This sets how many polygons your base mesh starts with. More polygons means more detail potential but a heavier file.
    • Low: Fewer polygons. Good for rough blockouts and practice.
    • Med: A balanced starting point for most sculpts.
    • High: Maximum polygon density. Use this when you know you'll need very fine detail (wrinkles, scales, tiny patterns).

Export

  • 🖨️ Printer Export: Exports your sculpt as an OBJ file suitable for 3D printing. This is how you get your ZBrush creation into Blender or into a slicer for printing. We'll use this extensively in later lessons.

Navigation — Using a Tablet

ZBrushCoreMini is designed to be used with a graphics tablet (like a Wacom or Huion tablet). The tablet's pen pressure affects brush behavior — pressing harder creates a stronger stroke. Here's how to navigate the 3D viewport:

Rotate the View

Click and drag outside the mesh (on the empty background area) to rotate around your sculpt. This lets you view it from any angle. Practice this until it feels natural — you'll be rotating constantly.

Pan (Move the View)

Alt + click and drag anywhere to pan the viewport, sliding the view left, right, up, or down without rotating.

Zoom

Ctrl + click and drag outside the mesh to zoom in and out. Drag upward to zoom in, downward to zoom out. You can also use the scroll wheel if your tablet or mouse has one.

Other Important Controls

Lighting

Light icon: Click and drag the light icon to change the direction the light comes from. Adjusting the lighting helps you see surface detail — shadows reveal bumps, ridges, and grooves that might be invisible under flat lighting. Move the light around as you sculpt to check your work from different lighting angles.

Undo / Redo

Ctrl + Z to undo your last stroke. You can undo many steps back. Ctrl + Shift + Z to redo (bring back something you just undid). Use undo liberally — sculpting is an experimental process, and undoing a stroke you don't like is completely normal.

Tips for Getting Started

  • Save often. There's no auto-save. Get in the habit of pressing the Save icon every few minutes.
  • Start with symmetry on. It's much easier to get balanced, professional-looking results when both sides match.
  • Rotate constantly. Always spin your model around to check how it looks from every angle. A detail that looks great from the front might look wrong from the side.
  • Start big, then go small. Block out the major forms first with big brushes and high intensity, then decrease your brush size and intensity for finer and finer detail.
  • Relax your grip. Let the tablet pen pressure do the work. A light touch with high Z-Intensity gives you more control than pressing hard with low intensity.
