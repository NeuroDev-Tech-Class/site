Intro to Unreal Engine

Landscapes & Terrain

Create, sculpt, and paint realistic outdoor terrain using Unreal Engine's Landscape system

Why Use Landscapes?

While you could build terrain out of static meshes (flat planes, ramps, cubes), Unreal Engine's Landscape system is purpose-built for large outdoor environments. It provides a single, continuous terrain surface that you can sculpt like digital clay and paint with multiple surface types — grass, dirt, rock, sand — all blending together seamlessly.

Step 1: Prepare Your Level

If your level already has a floor mesh (like the default floor plane in the Third Person template), you'll want to remove it first so it doesn't intersect with your landscape.

  1. Click the existing floor in the Viewport (or find it in the Outliner — it's usually named Floor or SM_Floor).
  2. Press Delete to remove it.

Step 2: Enter Landscape Mode

  1. In the Modes dropdown on the left side of the toolbar, select Landscape (or use the Shift+3 shortcut).
  2. The Landscape panel will appear, showing three tabs: Manage, Sculpt, and Paint.
  3. On the Manage tab, you'll see a preview grid in the Viewport showing where your landscape will be created.

Configuring the Landscape

Before clicking Create, you can adjust the size:

Setting What It Controls Recommendation
Section Size Resolution of each landscape section 63×63 for smaller maps, 127×127 for larger
Sections Per Component How many sections make up one component 1×1 is fine for most cases
Number of Components Overall size of the landscape grid 8×8 for a small scene, 16×16 or more for large worlds
Overall Resolution Total vertex count (auto-calculated) Keep it reasonable to maintain good performance
Tip: For this course, the default settings work well. Don't worry about making the landscape too large — you can always expand it later. A good starting size is around 505×505 overall resolution.

Click the green Create button. A flat landscape will appear in your level.

Step 3: Sculpt the Terrain

Switch to the Sculpt tab in the Landscape panel. You now have access to several sculpting tools:

Tool What It Does
Sculpt The primary tool. Click and drag to raise terrain. Hold Shift and drag to lower it.
Smooth Blends sharp edges and evens out bumpy areas
Flatten Brings terrain to a uniform height (great for building flat areas for structures)
Erosion Simulates natural erosion for realistic weathered terrain
Noise Adds random variation for organic-looking terrain

Adjust the Brush Size and Brush Strength sliders to control how large an area is affected and how much terrain moves per stroke. Start with a large brush and low strength for broad shapes, then switch to a smaller brush for fine details.

Sculpting Workflow:
  1. Block out major shapes — Use a large brush to create hills, valleys, and ridges.
  2. Refine with medium brush — Shape slopes, create cliffs, carve paths.
  3. Smooth everything — Switch to the Smooth tool to remove harsh edges.
  4. Add micro-detail — Small brush + Noise tool for natural-looking surface variation.

Step 4: Create a Landscape Material

A bare landscape is just gray geometry. To make it look like real terrain, you need a Landscape Material that supports multiple layers (e.g., grass, dirt, rock) and blends between them based on how you paint.

Creating the Material

  1. In the Content Browser, right-click → Material → name it M_Landscape.
  2. Double-click to open the Material Editor.
  3. Right-click in the graph and add a LandscapeLayerBlend node. This is the key node that lets you mix multiple texture layers.
  4. In the LandscapeLayerBlend details, click the + button to add layers. Create one for each surface type:
    • Grass
    • Dirt
    • Rock
  5. Set the Blend Type for each layer to LB Weight Blend.

Adding Textures

  1. For each layer, add a Texture Sample node and assign an appropriate texture (find grass, dirt, and rock textures in your StarterContent or imported assets).
  2. Connect each Texture Sample's output to the corresponding layer input on the LandscapeLayerBlend node.
  3. Connect the LandscapeLayerBlend output to the Base Color input on the main material node.

Setting Up UVs (Tiling)

Landscape textures need to tile properly. Without adjusting UVs, textures will appear stretched across the massive landscape surface.

  1. Add a LandscapeLayerCoords node for each texture layer.
  2. Set the Mapping Scale to control tiling — a value like 0.01 to 0.05 usually works well (smaller = more tiling = less stretching).
  3. Connect the LandscapeLayerCoords output to the UVs input on the corresponding Texture Sample.

Click Apply and Save in the Material Editor.

Assigning the Material

  1. Select the Landscape actor in the Viewport or Outliner.
  2. In the Details Panel, find the Landscape Material slot and assign your M_Landscape material.

Step 5: Paint Layers on the Landscape

  1. Switch to the Paint tab in the Landscape panel.
  2. You should see your layers listed (Grass, Dirt, Rock). If prompted to create layer info, click the + next to each layer and choose Weight-Blended Layer (Normal). Save them to your project's Content folder.
  3. Select a layer (e.g., Grass), then click and drag on the landscape in the Viewport to paint it.
  4. Switch layers and paint different areas — dirt for paths, rock for cliff faces, grass for meadows.
Tip: Use Brush Falloff for smooth blending between layers. A soft falloff creates natural-looking transitions, while a hard falloff creates sharp borders (useful for paths or road edges).
Performance Note: Landscapes with many layers and high-resolution textures can be expensive to render. For this course, 3–4 layers is plenty. If your editor starts to lag, reduce the landscape resolution or texture sizes.

Tips for Realistic Terrain

  • Reference real geography. Look at photos of the environment you're recreating — notice how grass grows on flat areas, dirt appears on slopes, and rock is exposed on steep cliffs.
  • Vary the height. Perfectly flat terrain looks artificial. Even a "flat" meadow has subtle rolls and dips.
  • Use the Erosion tool to simulate natural weathering on mountain slopes.
  • Layer painting follows landscape. Paint rock on steep areas and grass on flat — this mimics how real terrain layers naturally form.
  • Don't over-sculpt. Subtle is almost always better. Real terrain changes gradually over large distances.
Practice:
  1. Create a new landscape using the default settings.
  2. Sculpt a valley surrounded by hills — use the Sculpt tool to raise terrain on the edges and lower it in the center.
  3. Create a basic Landscape Material with at least two layers (Grass and Dirt).
  4. Paint grass in the valley and dirt on the hilltops.
  5. Place a Player Start in the valley and press Play to walk around your terrain.
