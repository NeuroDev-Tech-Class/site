Foliage & Detail

Quickly populate your world with trees, grass, rocks, and other environmental details

The Foliage Tool

Placing trees, bushes, and grass one at a time would take forever. Unreal Engine's Foliage tool lets you paint large quantities of static meshes onto surfaces with a brush — like spray-painting nature onto your landscape. In seconds, you can transform a barren terrain into a lush forest or a windswept meadow.

Getting Started

1. Enter Foliage Mode

Switch to Foliage Mode from the Modes dropdown in the toolbar (or press Shift+4). The Foliage panel will appear on the left side of the editor.

2. Add Meshes to the Foliage Palette

Before you can paint, you need to tell the tool what to paint:

  1. In the Foliage panel, click the + Add Foliage Type button (or drag static meshes from the Content Browser directly into the panel).
  2. Select one or more static meshes — trees, bushes, grass clumps, rocks, flowers, etc.
  3. Each mesh appears as a thumbnail in the palette with a checkbox. Only checked meshes will be painted.
Tip — Free Assets: Check the Epic Games Marketplace for free content packs. Epic regularly gives away asset packs — search for "free" in the Marketplace, or look for packs like Open World Demo Collection, Megascans Trees, or Stylized Nature Pack. These include high-quality foliage meshes ready to use.

3. Configure Brush Settings

At the top of the Foliage panel, adjust your brush:

Setting What It Controls Recommended Start
Brush Size Radius of the paint area Start medium (~2000–5000)
Paint Density How many instances are placed per stroke Start low (~0.1–0.3) and increase as needed
Erase Density How aggressively Shift+Paint removes foliage Default is fine

4. Paint!

  • Left-click and drag on your landscape (or any surface) to paint foliage.
  • Hold Shift and drag to erase foliage.
  • Instances are randomly scattered within the brush radius, creating a natural look automatically.

Per-Mesh Settings

Click on a mesh thumbnail in the palette to expand its individual settings. These let you fine-tune how each foliage type is placed:

Setting What It Does
Scale Min / Max Randomizes each instance's size within a range. E.g., set to 0.8 – 1.2 for subtle variety, or 0.5 – 1.5 for dramatic variation.
Random Yaw Randomly rotates each instance around the vertical axis so they don't all face the same direction.
Align to Normal Tilts instances to match the slope of the surface they're painted on.
Ground Slope Angle Limits placement to surfaces within a certain slope range — prevents trees from appearing on sheer cliffs.
Radius Minimum distance between instances. Higher values = more spread out, lower = more dense.
Cull Distance The maximum distance at which this foliage type is visible. Faraway grass can be culled for better performance.
Tip for Natural-Looking Foliage:
  • Layer different sizes: Paint large trees first (sparse), then medium bushes, then dense grass last.
  • Vary the scale: Even a ±20% size variation makes a huge difference in breaking up the uniformity.
  • Don't overdo it: In nature, plants cluster in groups with open space between them. Avoid painting everything at maximum density.
  • Mix mesh types: Check multiple different meshes in the palette and paint them together for natural variety.

Performance Considerations

Foliage can add up fast. Thousands of mesh instances — especially complex trees — can significantly impact frame rate. Keep these guidelines in mind:

  • Start with low density and increase only where needed.
  • Use Cull Distance to hide distant small foliage (grass, flowers) — the player won't notice they're missing at range.
  • Use LODs (Levels of Detail) if your meshes support them — simpler versions render at a distance.
  • Nanite can help with foliage meshes — enable it on meshes in their asset settings for automatic LOD handling.
  • Check your frame rate regularly. Press ` (backtick) to open the console, type stat fps, and press Enter to display the frame rate counter.
Warning: Dense grass foliage is one of the most performance-heavy things in any game. If your editor becomes sluggish after painting grass, reduce Paint Density and increase the Cull Distance Start value to remove grass at shorter distances.
Practice:
  1. Enter Foliage Mode (Shift+4).
  2. Add two or three different static meshes to the palette (trees and/or bushes from your Starter Content or a Marketplace pack).
  3. Set the Scale range to 0.8 – 1.2 for natural variety.
  4. Paint a cluster of trees on your landscape.
  5. Hold Shift and erase a clearing in the middle.
  6. Press Play and walk through your forest.
