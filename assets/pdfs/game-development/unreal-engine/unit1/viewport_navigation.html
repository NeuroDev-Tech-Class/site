Viewport Navigation

Master the controls for moving around 3D space and manipulating objects in the Unreal Editor

Moving Through the Viewport

The Viewport is your window into the 3D world. Learning to navigate it quickly and confidently is one of the most important skills you'll develop — everything you do in Unreal starts here. The controls may feel unfamiliar at first, but after a few minutes of practice they'll become second nature.

Camera Controls

The Viewport camera has several movement modes. The most common is Fly Mode, which works like a first-person camera:

  • Hold Right Mouse Button (RMB) and drag to look around (rotate the camera).
  • While still holding RMB, use WASD to fly forward, left, backward, and right.
  • While holding RMB, use Q to move down and E to move up.
  • Scroll the Mouse Wheel to zoom in and out (dolly the camera forward/back).
  • Hold Middle Mouse Button (MMB) and drag to pan the camera (slide left/right/up/down without rotating).
Tip — Camera Speed: If you feel like you're moving too slowly (or flying through walls), adjust the camera speed. Look at the top-right corner of the Viewport for a small speed slider, or hold RMB and scroll the Mouse Wheel to change speed on the fly.

Orbit Controls (Maya-Style)

If you prefer orbiting around a point (like rotating around a model you're inspecting), Unreal supports an orbit mode using the Alt key:

  • Alt + Left Mouse Button — Orbit (rotate around the focus point)
  • Alt + Right Mouse Button — Dolly (zoom toward/away from focus point)
  • Alt + Middle Mouse Button — Pan (slide the view)

Focus on Selection

One of the most useful shortcuts: select any actor and press F to focus the camera on it. The Viewport will snap to frame the selected object, and orbit controls will now rotate around it. This is invaluable when you lose track of a small object in a large scene.

Transform Gizmos

When you select an actor in the Viewport, a colorful transform gizmo appears on it. This gizmo lets you move, rotate, or scale the object. You switch between the three modes with keyboard shortcuts:

Tool Shortcut Gizmo Appearance What It Does
Move W Three arrows (red = X, green = Y, blue = Z) Drag an arrow to slide the actor along that axis
Rotate E Three colored rings Drag a ring to rotate around that axis
Scale R Three lines with cubes on the ends Drag a handle to stretch or shrink along that axis
Tip: You can also drag the small colored planes between axes (the squares near the center of the Move gizmo) to move along two axes at once — for example, dragging on the XY plane keeps the object at the same height.

Snapping

Snapping forces movements to fixed increments, making it easy to align objects precisely. The snapping controls are in the Viewport toolbar:

  • Grid Snap (the magnet icon) — toggle on/off. When enabled, objects move in set increments (e.g., 10 units at a time). Click the dropdown next to it to choose the grid size.
  • Rotation Snap — Rotations snap to fixed angles (e.g., every 10°).
  • Scale Snap — Scale changes snap to fixed increments.

You can quickly toggle snapping on and off while working — leave it on for architecture and level layout (walls, floors, pillars) and turn it off for organic placement (rocks, foliage, decorations).

World Space vs. Local Space

By default, the transform gizmo is aligned to World Space — the X, Y, and Z axes stay fixed no matter how an object is rotated. You can switch to Local Space to align the gizmo to the object's own orientation. This is essential when you want to push a rotated wall "forward" along its own face instead of along the world grid. Toggle between them using the globe/cube icon in the Viewport toolbar.

Practice: Open a project and try the following:
  1. Fly around the scene using RMB + WASD. Adjust the camera speed until it feels comfortable.
  2. Select an actor and press F to focus on it. Orbit around it with Alt + LMB.
  3. Switch between Move (W), Rotate (E), and Scale (R). Move an object, rotate it 45°, and scale it up.
  4. Duplicate an object with Alt + Drag.
  5. Enable grid snapping and place four cubes in a perfect row.
