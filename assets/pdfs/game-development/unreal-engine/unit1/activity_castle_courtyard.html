Activity: Castle Courtyard

Build a complete 3D environment by combining everything you've learned in Unit 1

Overview

In this guided activity, you'll create a castle courtyard environment from scratch. This project brings together landscapes, meshes, materials, lighting, and foliage — all the skills from Unit 1. By the end, you'll have a playable scene you can walk around in.

Before You Start: Make sure you have a content pack with architectural meshes (walls, pillars, arches). Good free options from the Epic Marketplace include Medieval Dungeon, Infinity Blade: Castle, or Soul: Cave. You can also use the default StarterContent architecture pieces — they won't look medieval, but the process is the same.

Step 1: Create a New Level

  1. Go to File → New Level.
  2. Choose Open World or Basic (either works — Open World includes sky and lighting by default).
  3. Save the level immediately: Ctrl+Shift+S and name it CastleCourtyard.

Step 2: Create & Sculpt the Landscape

  1. Delete any existing floor mesh.
  2. Enter Landscape Mode (Shift+3).
  3. Use the default settings and click Create.
  4. Switch to the Sculpt tab.
  5. Use a large brush with medium strength to raise terrain around the edges, creating a valley or basin in the center. This valley will be your courtyard area.
  6. Create some gentle slopes and hills around the exterior — avoid perfectly uniform walls of terrain.
  7. Use the Smooth tool to soften any harsh edges.
  8. Optionally, use the Flatten tool on the center area to create a nice flat surface for building.
Tip: The courtyard floor doesn't need to be perfectly flat — a tiny bit of variation looks more natural. But it should be level enough that walls and structures sit flush on it.

Step 3: Build the Castle Walls

  1. Switch back to Select Mode (press Shift+1 or click the cursor icon).
  2. Open the Content Browser (Ctrl+Space) and navigate to your architectural mesh assets.
  3. Drag wall segments into the Viewport. Position them along the edge of the valley to form the outer walls of your castle.
  4. Use Grid Snap to align wall pieces edge-to-edge — no gaps.
  5. Duplicate walls with Alt+Drag to speed up the process.
  6. Add corner pieces or towers at the corners. Rotate them as needed (E).
  7. Leave an opening in one wall for the main gate / entrance.
  8. Optionally add pillars, arches, stairs, or a central fountain inside the courtyard.
Keep It Organized: As you add meshes, name them in the Outliner (double-click to rename). Create folders like "Walls", "Props", "Towers" to keep things tidy. A messy Outliner makes editing much harder later.

Step 4: Paint the Landscape

  1. Create a Landscape Material with at least 2–3 layers:
    • Grass — for the courtyard interior and surrounding meadows
    • Dirt / Stone — for paths, around walls, and high-traffic areas
    • Rock (optional) — for steep hill areas and cliff faces
  2. Assign the material to your landscape in the Details Panel.
  3. Enter Landscape Mode → Paint and create layer info for each layer.
  4. Paint grass inside the courtyard and on the gentle hills.
  5. Paint dirt/stone around the walls, along pathways, and at the gate entrance.
  6. Paint rock on steep slopes if applicable.

Step 5: Add Foliage

  1. Enter Foliage Mode (Shift+4).
  2. Add tree and bush meshes to the foliage palette.
  3. Paint trees and bushes on the exterior hills — around the outside of the castle walls.
  4. Add a few trees or decorative bushes inside the courtyard for life.
  5. Use Shift + paint to erase any foliage that clips through walls or appears in unwanted spots.
  6. Set scale variation to 0.8 – 1.3 for natural variety.

Step 6: Set Up Lighting

Create a warm, sunset atmosphere:

  1. Select the Directional Light and rotate it so the sun sits low on the horizon (around 15–25° above the ground plane).
  2. Set the Light Color to a warm orange tone (e.g., R: 255, G: 200, B: 140).
  3. Adjust Intensity to your liking — slightly lower than default for a golden-hour feel.
  4. Select the Exponential Height Fog and set its Inscattering Color to a complementary warm tone. Enable Volumetric Fog for god rays through the gate.
  5. Add a Post Process Volume (set to Infinite Extent) and subtly increase:
    • Bloom — slight glow around bright areas
    • Vignette — gentle darkening at screen edges
    • Color Grading — slightly boost Saturation and add a warm tint to the shadows

Step 7: Place the Player Start

  1. Add a Player Start actor (+ → Basic → Player Start).
  2. Place it inside the courtyard on a flat surface.
  3. Point the blue arrow toward something interesting — the gate, a fountain, or down a path.
  4. Make sure the Player Start is not clipping into any geometry (the icon should be green, not red).

Step 8: Play-Test!

  1. Press Play (Alt+P) to enter the game.
  2. Walk around the courtyard. Look for:
    • Gaps between wall pieces
    • Foliage clipping through walls
    • Areas where the player can fall through the landscape
    • Lighting and atmosphere — does it feel right?
  3. Press Esc to stop play-testing.
  4. Go back and fix any issues you noticed.

Completion Checklist

✓ Requirement
☐ Landscape created with sculpted terrain (valley/hills)
☐ Castle walls placed around the courtyard with at least one entrance
☐ Landscape painted with at least 2 layers (grass + dirt/stone)
☐ Foliage painted on exterior terrain (trees, bushes)
☐ Lighting set to a warm sunset atmosphere
☐ Post Process Volume with at least one visible effect
☐ Player Start placed inside the courtyard
☐ Play-tested and walked through the level successfully
☐ Outliner is organized with named actors and/or folders
☐ Level saved (Ctrl+S)
Going Further: If you finish early, try adding interior details: banners on the walls, torches with Point Lights, a well or fountain in the center, or crates and barrels near the gate. The more detail you add, the more immersive the environment becomes.
