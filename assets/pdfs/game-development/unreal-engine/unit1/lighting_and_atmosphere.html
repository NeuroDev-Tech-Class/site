Lighting & Atmosphere

Set the mood of your world with lights, sky, fog, and post-processing effects

Why Lighting Matters

Lighting is one of the single biggest factors that determines how your game feels. The same environment can look like a cheerful meadow under bright daylight or a foreboding wasteland under overcast skies — all depending on how you set up your lights. UE5's lighting system is incredibly powerful, and it starts with just a handful of key actors.

The Core Light Actors

Directional Light (The Sun)

The Directional Light simulates a light source that is infinitely far away — like the sun. Its rays are parallel, and it illuminates the entire scene from a single direction.

  • Rotation controls the time of day — rotate it to change the angle of the sun. A low angle creates long shadows (sunrise/sunset), while a high angle creates short shadows (noon).
  • Intensity controls brightness (measured in lux).
  • Light Color lets you tint the sunlight — warm orange for golden hour, cool blue for overcast.
  • Enable Atmosphere Sun Light in the Details Panel to make this light drive the sky color (golden sky at sunset, blue at noon).

Sky Light

The Sky Light captures the environment around your level and uses it to fill in ambient lighting — the soft, indirect light that reaches shaded areas. Without a Sky Light, shadows would be completely black.

  • Set the Source Type to SLS Captured Scene so it automatically uses the current sky as its light source.
  • After making changes to the sky or Directional Light, click Recapture Sky in the Sky Light's Details Panel to update the ambient lighting.
  • Adjust the Intensity Scale to make ambient light brighter or darker.

Sky Atmosphere

The Sky Atmosphere actor renders a physically accurate sky model. It simulates Rayleigh and Mie scattering — the real-world physics that make the sky blue during the day and orange at sunset.

  • It works in conjunction with the Directional Light — as you rotate the sun, the sky colors update automatically.
  • You can tweak scattering parameters to create alien-looking skies (e.g., a green atmosphere for a sci-fi planet).

Volumetric Clouds

UE5 includes a Volumetric Cloud actor that renders realistic, fully 3D clouds. These aren't just flat skybox images — they have depth, catch light from the sun, and cast shadows on the ground.

  • Adjust Layer Bottom Altitude and Layer Height to control where clouds appear.
  • Customize cloud density and shape through the cloud material settings.
Tip: Most template projects already include a Directional Light, Sky Light, Sky Atmosphere, and Volumetric Clouds. Check the Outliner before adding new ones — having duplicates can cause visual artifacts.

Exponential Height Fog

Exponential Height Fog adds atmospheric haze that becomes denser closer to the ground and thinner at higher altitudes. It's essential for creating distance, depth, and mood.

  • Fog Density controls overall thickness.
  • Fog Height Falloff controls how quickly the fog fades as altitude increases.
  • Inscattering Color sets the fog color — match it to your sky for a cohesive look.
  • Enable Volumetric Fog (checkbox in the Details Panel) for light shafts (god rays) when sunlight pierces through the fog.

Lumen: Real-Time Global Illumination

One of UE5's headline features is Lumen, a fully dynamic global illumination and reflection system. In previous Unreal versions, realistic lighting required long bake times — pre-computing how light bounces off surfaces. Lumen does this in real time.

What this means for you:

  • Move a wall, and the light that bounces off it updates instantly.
  • Place a bright red carpet in a white room, and the walls will pick up a subtle red tint from bounced light.
  • Reflections on shiny surfaces show the actual surrounding scene, not just a static capture.
  • No baking required — iterate faster and see results immediately.
Hardware Note: Lumen requires a relatively modern GPU to run at full quality. If your frame rate drops in the editor, you can switch the Global Illumination method back to Screen Space or None under Project Settings → Engine → Rendering → Global Illumination. You can also lower Lumen quality in Engine Scalability Settings.

Static vs. Dynamic Lighting

Every light actor has a Mobility setting in the Details Panel:

Mobility Description Best For
Static Pre-baked. Cannot change at runtime. Highest quality shadows but requires baking. Indoor scenes with fixed lighting on lower-end hardware
Stationary Baked indirect light, real-time direct light. Can change intensity/color at runtime. Sun lights that shift gradually (day/night cycle)
Movable Fully dynamic. Lumen handles the indirect bounce. Most flexible, higher GPU cost. Default for UE5 projects using Lumen
Tip: When using Lumen, set your lights to Movable. Lumen is designed around fully dynamic lights and works best that way. You only need Static lights if you're targeting very low-end hardware or mobile platforms.

Post Process Volume

A Post Process Volume applies screen-space effects to the camera — these are finishing touches applied after the scene is rendered, like filters on a photo.

Common post-process settings:

Effect What It Does
Bloom Makes bright areas glow softly — mimics how real cameras behave
Exposure (Auto/Manual) Controls how the camera adapts to brightness — important for indoor/outdoor transitions
Color Grading Adjust contrast, saturation, tint, shadows, highlights — like color correction in film
Ambient Occlusion Adds soft shadows in crevices and corners for added depth
Vignette Darkens the edges of the screen for a cinematic feel
Depth of Field Blurs distant or close objects to simulate camera focus

To use a Post Process Volume:

  1. Add one from + → Visual Effects → Post Process Volume.
  2. In the Details Panel, check Infinite Extent (Unbound) to apply the effect globally, or leave it unchecked to only affect the player when they're inside the volume's box.
  3. Adjust settings to taste — start subtle and build up.

Performance Tips

  • One Directional Light is enough — multiple directional lights are expensive and rarely needed.
  • Use Movable lights with Lumen — mixing Static and Movable lights in the same scene with Lumen can cause inconsistencies.
  • Lower the Volumetric Cloud quality in scalability settings if you're experiencing frame drops.
  • Turn off Volumetric Fog on Exponential Height Fog if you don't need god rays — it's one of the more expensive effects.
  • Post Process effects stack up. Each effect adds to the GPU cost. On older hardware, disable Depth of Field and reduce Bloom intensity.
Practice:
  1. Open a project and locate the Directional Light in the Outliner.
  2. Rotate it with the Rotate tool (E) to simulate different times of day. Watch how the Sky Atmosphere updates.
  3. Select the Exponential Height Fog and enable Volumetric Fog. Observe the god rays.
  4. Add a Post Process Volume, set it to Infinite Extent, and experiment with Bloom, Color Grading, and Vignette.
  5. Try changing the Directional Light color to a warm orange and setting the exposure manually for a sunset look.
