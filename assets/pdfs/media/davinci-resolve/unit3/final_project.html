Video Production — DaVinci Resolve

Final Project: Short Film or Video Essay

Create a polished 3–5 minute video that demonstrates your editing, color, and audio skills

Overview

For your final project, you will plan, edit, and deliver a 3–5 minute video that showcases everything you've learned in this course. This is your chance to combine editing fundamentals, color grading, audio mixing, and creative storytelling into a cohesive, finished piece.

Choose Your Format

Select one of the following formats for your project:

Option A: Short Narrative Film

Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end. This could be a scripted scene, a short drama, a comedy sketch, or a silent visual story. You can act in it yourself, recruit friends or family, or use creative techniques like voice-over narration with B-roll footage. Focus on pacing, shot variety, and mood.

Option B: Video Essay

Create an informative or argumentative video about a topic you're interested in. Video essays combine narration (voice-over or on-camera) with supporting visuals — stock footage, screen recordings, images, text overlays, and graphics. Think YouTube educational content. Focus on clear communication, visual variety, and engaging pacing.

Option C: Mini Documentary

Document a person, place, process, or subject that interests you. Interview someone, capture B-roll of their environment or work, and weave it into a short documentary. You could profile a friend's hobby, document a local business, explore your neighborhood, or cover an event. Focus on authentic storytelling and visual variety.

Tip: Choose a format that excites you. The best projects come from genuine interest in the subject matter. If you're not sure, the Video Essay is the most approachable — you can work with a topic you already know and use stock footage to supplement your visuals.

Requirements

Regardless of which format you choose, your project must meet all of the following requirements:

Content Requirements

  • Duration: 3–5 minutes
  • Title sequence: An opening title with your video's name (using Text+ or a Fusion title)
  • Clips: At least 15 edited clips on the timeline (this includes any B-roll, cutaways, or supplementary footage)
  • Transitions: At least 5 transitions used purposefully (not randomly placed)
  • Audio: Background music and/or sound effects (royalty-free). Audio levels properly balanced
  • Text overlays: Lower thirds, labels, titles, or other text elements as appropriate for your content

Technical Requirements

  • DaVinci Resolve Pages: Use at least 3 pages — Edit + Color + Deliver at minimum. Fairlight and/or Fusion count as additional pages.
  • Color correction: All clips must be color corrected on the Color page (exposure, white balance). Clips should look consistent.
  • Color grade: Apply a creative grade that supports the mood of your video
  • Keyframe animation: At least 1 keyframed animation (zoom, pan, rotation, opacity, text animation, etc.)
  • Organized Media Pool: Clips organized into labeled bins (not all dumped in the root)
  • Export: Delivered as an MP4 at 1080p or higher, H.264 or H.265 codec

Written Companion

Write a 1-paragraph description (4–6 sentences) of your creative vision. Explain:

  • What your video is about and why you chose this topic/story
  • What mood or feeling you wanted to create
  • What creative choices you made in editing, color, and audio to support that vision

Submit this paragraph as a text file or document alongside your video.

Suggested Workflow

Phase 1: Pre-Production (Planning)

  1. Choose your format (narrative, essay, or documentary).
  2. Choose your topic. Write a one-sentence summary of what your video is about.
  3. Plan your shots/content. Make a simple list or outline of what footage you need. For a video essay, outline your script and what visuals will accompany each point.
  4. Gather footage: Record original footage and/or download royalty-free clips. Download music and sound effects.

Phase 2: Production (Editing)

  1. Create a new project in DaVinci Resolve with proper settings (1080p, matching frame rate).
  2. Import and organize all media into labeled bins.
  3. Build your rough cut: Arrange clips on the timeline in order. Don't worry about perfection — get the structure right first.
  4. Refine the edit: Trim clips, adjust pacing, remove dead space. Add B-roll and cutaways.
  5. Add transitions between clips where appropriate.
  6. Add titles and text: Opening title, lower thirds, labels, end credits.
  7. Add and mix audio: Import music to an audio track, add sound effects, balance levels. Ensure dialogue (if any) is clear above the music.
  8. Add keyframe animations: Apply at least one animated element (zoom, pan, text slide-in, opacity fade).

Phase 3: Post-Production (Polish)

  1. Color correct every clip on the Color page. Fix exposure and white balance. Match shots for consistency.
  2. Apply a creative grade that supports your video's mood.
  3. Final audio pass: Listen through with headphones. Check for pops, clicks, sudden volume changes, or clipping.
  4. Watch from start to finish at least twice. Make notes and fix issues.
  5. Export via the Deliver page: MP4, H.264, 1080p or higher.

Phase 4: Submission

  1. Your exported MP4 video file
  2. Your written creative vision paragraph
