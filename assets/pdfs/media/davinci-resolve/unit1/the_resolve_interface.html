Video Production — DaVinci Resolve

The Resolve Interface

Understanding DaVinci Resolve's page-based workflow and the Edit page layout

A Page-Based Workflow

DaVinci Resolve organizes its entire workflow into pages. Instead of cramming every tool into a single window, Resolve gives each major task its own dedicated workspace. You switch between pages using the icons at the very bottom of the screen.

This approach keeps the interface uncluttered — you only see the tools relevant to what you're doing right now. As you progress through a project, you naturally move from page to page: import media, edit, add effects, color grade, mix audio, and export.

The 7 Pages

From left to right at the bottom of the screen, you'll find these page icons:

1. Media

The Media page is where your project begins. Use it to browse your computer's file system, import footage and audio, and organize clips into bins (folders). You can preview clips, read metadata, and set up your media library before you start editing. Think of it as your project's filing cabinet.

2. Cut

The Cut page is designed for fast, efficient editing. It features a dual-timeline view — a zoomed-out overview and a zoomed-in detail view — that lets you quickly assemble a rough cut without a lot of precise navigation. It's ideal for quick turnarounds, vlogs, and social media content where speed matters more than fine-tuning.

3. Edit

The Edit page is the traditional NLE workspace and where you'll spend most of your time in this course. It provides a full-featured timeline, source/record viewer workflow, effects library, and detailed control over every clip. If you've used Premiere Pro or Final Cut, this page will feel familiar.

4. Fusion

The Fusion page is a node-based visual effects and motion graphics compositor built right into Resolve. Instead of stacking layers, you connect nodes in a flow graph to create complex effects like green screen composites, animated titles, particle effects, and 3D text. It's incredibly powerful — and we'll introduce it in Unit 2.

5. Color

The Color page is what made DaVinci Resolve famous. It's the industry standard for color correction and grading. With color wheels, curves, scopes, qualifiers, Power Windows, and a node-based grading pipeline, this page gives you Hollywood-level color control. We'll dive deep into it in Unit 2.

6. Fairlight

The Fairlight page is a full digital audio workstation (DAW) embedded in Resolve. It provides a professional mixing console, EQ, dynamics processing, audio effects, automation, and support for recording directly into your project. If your video has dialogue, music, or sound effects, Fairlight is where you polish the audio.

7. Deliver

The Deliver page is your final stop. Here you configure your export settings — format, codec, resolution, frame rate, and quality — and render your finished video. It includes presets for YouTube, Vimeo, and other platforms, or you can create fully custom export configurations. You'll use this page at the end of every project.

Tip: You can switch pages at any time by clicking the icons at the bottom of the screen, or by using the keyboard shortcuts Shift+2 through Shift+8 (Media through Deliver).

The Edit Page Layout

Since the Edit page is where you'll do most of your work, let's break down its layout in detail.

Media Pool (Top-Left)

The Media Pool panel shows all the clips, audio files, and images you've imported into your project. You can organize them into bins (folders) by right-clicking and selecting New Bin. Drag clips from the Media Pool to the timeline or double-click to load them into the Source Viewer.

Source Viewer (Top-Left)

When you double-click a clip in the Media Pool, it opens in the Source Viewer. Here you can preview the clip before adding it to your timeline. You can set In and Out points to select just the portion you want, then insert or overwrite it into the timeline.

Timeline Viewer (Top-Right)

The Timeline Viewer (also called the Record Viewer or Program Viewer) shows what your edited sequence looks like at the current playhead position. As you play through your timeline, this viewer displays the output — exactly what your audience will see.

Timeline (Bottom)

The Timeline panel stretches across the bottom of the Edit page. This is the heart of editing — where you arrange your clips on video and audio tracks, trim them, add transitions, and build your project. Tracks are stacked vertically (V1, V2 for video; A1, A2 for audio), and time flows left to right.

Inspector (Top-Right Panel)

The Inspector appears on the right side of the screen. When you select a clip in the timeline, the Inspector shows its properties: Transform (position, zoom, rotation), Cropping, Compositing (opacity, blend mode), Speed, Stabilization, and more. It's also where you adjust text properties, transition settings, and effect parameters.

Effects Library (Top-Left, Togglable)

The Effects Library panel contains all of Resolve's built-in effects, transitions, titles, and generators. Access it from the top-left toolbar. It's organized into categories: Video Transitions, Audio Transitions, Titles, Generators, and Effects (filters). Drag any item from the Effects Library onto a clip or the timeline to apply it.

Key Toolbar Buttons

The Edit page toolbar runs along the top-left area and includes toggle buttons for showing/hiding panels:

  • Media Pool — toggle the clip browser
  • Effects Library — toggle effects, transitions, and titles
  • Edit Index — shows a list of all edits/markers in your timeline
  • Sound Library — browse built-in sound effects (Fairlight Sound Library)
  • Inspector — toggle the properties panel on the right
  • Metadata — view and edit clip metadata
  • Mixer — show the audio mixer within the Edit page

Customizing the Workspace

DaVinci Resolve gives you flexibility to arrange the Edit page to suit your preferences:

  • Resize panels by dragging the borders between them. Make the timeline taller or give the viewers more space — whatever helps your workflow.
  • Show/hide panels using the toolbar buttons described above. If you don't need the Effects Library, close it to gain more room.
  • Dual-screen mode: If you have two monitors, go to Workspace > Dual Screen > On. Resolve will put the viewers on one screen and the timeline on the other.
  • Full-screen viewer: Press Shift+F to expand the viewer to full screen for a better preview. Press it again to return.
  • Save workspace layouts: Go to Workspace > Layout Presets > Save Layout as Preset to save your custom arrangement and recall it later.
Tip: When starting out, keep the default layout. Once you're comfortable with where everything is, start customizing to match how you like to work.

Key Takeaways

  • DaVinci Resolve uses a page-based workflow with 7 dedicated pages: Media, Cut, Edit, Fusion, Color, Fairlight, and Deliver.
  • The Edit page is your primary workspace — it contains the Media Pool, Source Viewer, Timeline Viewer, Timeline, Inspector, and Effects Library.
  • You can toggle panels on and off, resize them, and save custom layouts to suit your editing style.
  • Use the page icons at the bottom of the screen (or Shift+number shortcuts) to move between pages.
