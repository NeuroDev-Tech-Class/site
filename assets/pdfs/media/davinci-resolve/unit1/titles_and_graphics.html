Titles & Graphics

Adding text, lower thirds, compound clips, and adjustment layers to your projects

Adding Text to Your Video

Text overlays are essential for titles, credits, captions, labels, and lower thirds (the name/title graphics you see at the bottom of interview footage). In DaVinci Resolve, text is added through the Effects Library.

Text+ Title

Text+ is the primary text tool in DaVinci Resolve. It's powerful and flexible, built on the Fusion engine. To add it:

  1. Open the Effects Library on the Edit page.
  2. Navigate to Titles.
  3. Find Text+ and drag it to a video track above your footage (e.g., V2 if your video is on V1).
  4. Position and trim the text clip on the timeline to control when it appears and disappears.

Customizing Text in the Inspector

Select the Text+ clip on the timeline, then open the Inspector. You'll find extensive customization options:

PropertyWhat It Controls
TextThe actual text content — type your title here
FontTypeface selection (any font installed on your system)
SizeFont size
ColorText fill color
Position (Center X/Y)Where the text appears on screen
TrackingSpacing between individual letters
Line SpacingSpacing between lines of multi-line text

Drop Shadow

A drop shadow adds a dark shadow behind your text, making it pop against any background. In the Inspector under the Shading tab, enable the shadow element and adjust offset, softness, and opacity. This is especially useful when your text sits over bright or busy footage.

Outline / Stroke

An outline (also called a stroke) draws a border around each letter. Under the Shading tab in the Inspector, you can configure separate shading layers — use one for the main fill color and another for an outline by setting the element type to "Outline." Adjust the thickness and color to your taste.

Background

Some title templates include a solid or semi-transparent background behind the text (like a colored bar). For Text+, you can add a background by enabling a shading element set to "Background" under the Shading tab. This creates a rectangular box behind the text — useful for ensuring readability.

Tip: Choose fonts carefully. Stick to clean, readable fonts for professional work. Sans-serif fonts like Arial, Helvetica, Montserrat, or Open Sans work well for on-screen titles.

Lower Thirds

A lower third is a graphic that appears in the bottom third of the screen, typically showing a person's name and title. DaVinci Resolve includes pre-built lower third templates:

  1. In the Effects Library, go to Titles.
  2. Look for templates labeled Lower Third (e.g., "Lower Third," "Left Lower Third," "Scroll").
  3. Drag the template onto a track above your video.
  4. Select it and use the Inspector to change the name, title text, colors, and animation timing.

These templates come with built-in animations — the text slides in and out automatically. You can adjust the animation duration and style in the Inspector.

Compound Clips

A compound clip combines multiple timeline clips into a single container. This is useful when you have a complex arrangement (e.g., a title with a background and animation on multiple tracks) and want to treat it as one unit.

To create a compound clip:

  1. Select all the clips you want to combine (hold Ctrl and click each one, or drag a selection box).
  2. Right-click and choose New Compound Clip.
  3. Give it a name and click Create.

The selected clips are replaced by a single compound clip on the timeline. To edit the contents later, double-click the compound clip to open it in its own timeline. Changes inside are reflected everywhere the compound clip is used.

Tip: Compound clips are great for reusable elements like intro sequences. Build your intro once, create a compound clip, and drop it into any timeline.

Adjustment Layers

An Adjustment Clip (adjustment layer) applies effects to everything on the tracks below it. It's like placing a transparent filter over your footage.

  1. In the Effects Library, go to Effects (not Titles or Transitions).
  2. Find Adjustment Clip and drag it to a video track above your footage.
  3. Stretch the Adjustment Clip to cover the clips you want to affect.
  4. Select the Adjustment Clip and apply effects through the Inspector or by dragging effects onto it.

Use cases for adjustment layers:

  • Apply a color grade or filter to multiple clips at once
  • Add a vignette or blur effect across an entire section
  • Create a letterbox (cinematic black bars) that spans your whole timeline

Simple Motion Graphics with Keyframes

You can create engaging motion graphics by keyframing Text+ properties:

  1. Add a Text+ title to the timeline.
  2. Select it and go to the Inspector.
  3. Move the playhead to the start of the text clip.
  4. Set Center Y to a value off-screen (e.g., below the visible frame) and click the diamond keyframe icon.
  5. Move the playhead forward about 0.5–1 second.
  6. Change Center Y to place the text in its final position. A new keyframe is created.
  7. Add Ease In and Out to the keyframes for a smooth slide.

This creates a text that slides up into position. You can animate any property — opacity (fade in), size (grow), tracking (letters spread apart), or rotation (spin in).

Key Takeaways

  • Use Text+ from the Effects Library > Titles for on-screen text. Place it on a track above your video.
  • Customize font, size, color, position, shadow, and outline in the Inspector.
  • Lower Third templates provide pre-animated name/title graphics for interviews and presentations.
  • Compound Clips bundle multiple clips into one for easier management.
  • Adjustment Clips apply effects to everything below them on the timeline.
  • Combine Text+ with keyframes to create simple but effective motion graphics.
