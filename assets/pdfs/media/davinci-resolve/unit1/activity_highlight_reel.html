Video Production — DaVinci Resolve

Activity: Highlight Reel

Put your Unit 1 skills together by building a polished 60–90 second highlight reel

Overview

In this activity you will create a highlight reel — a short, visually engaging montage that showcases a variety of clips set to music. This is a staple of video editing: music videos, travel montages, sports highlights, and showreels all follow this format.

You'll use every skill covered in Unit 1: importing and organizing media, editing on the timeline, adding transitions, creating titles, changing speed, and animating with keyframes.

Finding Footage

You can use your own footage (phone recordings, screen captures, camera footage) or download royalty-free clips from these sources:

  • Pexels Videos — pexels.com/videos — free stock video, no attribution required
  • Pixabay Videos — pixabay.com/videos — free stock video
  • Coverr — coverr.co — free cinematic stock footage

For royalty-free music:

  • Pixabay Music — pixabay.com/music
  • Free Music Archive — freemusicarchive.org
  • YouTube Audio Library — available inside YouTube Studio
Warning: Never use copyrighted music or video in your projects without permission. Stick to royalty-free sources listed above.

Requirements

Your highlight reel must meet all of the following requirements:

  • Duration: 60–90 seconds
  • Clips: At least 8 different clips
  • Transitions: At least 3 transitions (at least one must be a Cross Dissolve)
  • Title Text: A title at the beginning of the video (using Text+)
  • End Card: A closing screen with credits or a "thank you" message
  • Background Music: At least 1 royalty-free music track on an audio track
  • Speed Change: At least 1 clip with a speed change (slow motion or fast forward)
  • Keyframe Animation: At least 1 keyframed animation (zoom, pan, rotation, or opacity fade)

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1: Download Your Clips

Visit the stock video sites above and download at least 8–10 clips. Choose a cohesive theme if possible (nature, city life, sports, technology, etc.). Download at 1080p or higher. Also download a music track that fits the mood.

Step 2: Create a Project & Import Media

  1. Open DaVinci Resolve and create a new project.
  2. Open Project Settings (gear icon) and set Timeline Resolution to 1920×1080 and Timeline Frame Rate to match your footage (usually 30 fps).
  3. Go to the Media page and import all your clips and the music file.
  4. Create bins to organize your media: Video, Audio.

Step 3: Create Your Timeline

  1. Switch to the Edit page.
  2. Drag your first clip to the timeline area to create a timeline.
  3. Continue adding clips using Append to End (Shift+F12) or by dragging them to the timeline.

Step 4: Arrange and Trim Clips

  1. Rearrange clips by dragging them on the timeline until the order feels right.
  2. Trim each clip to show only the best parts — aim for 5–15 seconds per clip.
  3. Use the Blade tool (B) to cut away unwanted sections and Ripple Delete to close gaps.
  4. Ensure total duration is between 60 and 90 seconds.

Step 5: Add Transitions

  1. Add a Cross Dissolve between at least one pair of clips (Ctrl+T at the edit point).
  2. Add at least 2 more transitions of your choice from the Effects Library.
  3. Open a Dip to Black at the very end of the timeline for a clean fade-out.

Step 6: Add Title and End Card

  1. Drag Text+ from Effects Library > Titles onto V2 at the very beginning.
  2. In the Inspector, type your video's title. Choose a clean font and adjust size, color, and position.
  3. Create an end card: add another Text+ at the end with credits or a closing message. Optionally place it over a Dip to Black or a final still frame.

Step 7: Add Background Music

  1. Drag your music file from the Media Pool to audio track A2 (keep clip audio on A1 if applicable).
  2. Trim the music to fit the length of your video.
  3. If the music needs to fade out at the end, select the clip, go to the Inspector, and keyframe the Volume — or drag the audio fade handle at the end of the clip.

Step 8: Adjust Audio Levels

  1. If your video clips have audio you want to keep, adjust the balance so music doesn't overpower it.
  2. If you're using only music (no dialogue), mute the original clip audio by right-clicking each clip and choosing Unlink, then deleting the audio portion — or simply lower clip audio volume to minimum.
  3. Ensure music volume is at a comfortable level (around -12 dB to -6 dB on the meters).

Step 9: Add Speed Change & Keyframe Animation

  1. Pick one clip and apply a speed change — try slowing it to 50% for a dramatic slow-motion moment that matches the music.
  2. Pick one clip (or the title) and add a keyframe animation: a slow zoom-in, a pan across the frame, or a fade-in using opacity keyframes.
  3. Add Ease In and Out to the keyframes for a polished feel.

Step 10: Export

  1. Go to the Deliver page.
  2. Select the YouTube preset (or set Format to MP4, Codec to H.264, Resolution 1920×1080).
  3. Choose a file name and destination.
  4. Click Add to Render Queue, then Start Render.
