Introduction to Fusion

An overview of DaVinci Resolve's node-based compositing and visual effects engine

What Is Compositing?

Compositing is the process of combining multiple visual elements into a single image or sequence. Every time you see a superhero flying through a city, a weather map behind a news anchor, or an explosion added to a movie scene — that's compositing. Elements from different sources (live-action footage, CGI renders, still images, text, effects) are layered and blended together to create a seamless final result.

DaVinci Resolve includes Fusion, a professional-grade compositing application, built right into the software. Fusion has been used on feature films and high-end commercials for decades, and you get it for free.

Node-Based vs. Layer-Based

If you've used After Effects or the timeline in the Edit page, you're familiar with layer-based compositing — you stack elements on top of each other, and the top layer covers the ones below. It's intuitive for simple work.

Node-based compositing (used by Fusion) works differently. Instead of layers, you have a flow graph of connected nodes. Each node performs one operation (bring in media, merge two elements, apply a blur, add text, etc.), and you connect them with lines to define the data flow. The output of one node flows into the input of the next.

Why Nodes?

  • Non-destructive: You can insert, remove, or rearrange operations at any point without rebuilding everything.
  • Reusable: You can branch a single output to multiple inputs, processing the same source in different ways simultaneously.
  • Scalable: Complex compositions with dozens of elements remain organized and readable — much cleaner than a 50-layer stack.
  • Flexible: Any property on any node can be animated or connected to expressions and modifiers.
Tip: The node-based approach may feel unfamiliar at first, but once you understand the flow (left to right, source to output), it becomes very logical. Give it time — it's worth learning.

Accessing Fusion

There are two ways to work with Fusion:

  • Fusion page: Click the Fusion icon at the bottom of the screen (or press Shift+5). This opens the full Fusion workspace for the clip currently under the playhead on the Edit page timeline.
  • Fusion Composition: On the Edit page, go to Effects Library > Effects > Fusion Composition and drag it onto the timeline. This creates a blank Fusion comp you can build from scratch.

The Fusion Interface

Node Editor (Flow Graph)

The Node Editor is the large central panel. This is where you build your composition by adding nodes and connecting them. Each node appears as a small rectangle with input and output triangles. Lines between nodes show the flow of data.

Viewers

Fusion has two viewers (left and right) at the top. You can assign any node to either viewer to preview its output. Click a node and press 1 to send it to the left viewer, or 2 for the right. This lets you compare two stages of your composition side by side.

Inspector / Controls

Select any node, and the Inspector panel on the right shows that node's properties. You can adjust parameters, keyframe values, and connect modifiers here — just like the Inspector on the Edit page.

Spline Editor & Keyframe Editor

Below the Node Editor, you can open the Spline Editor (for animation curves) or the Keyframe Editor (for managing keyframes over time). These work like animation curve editors in other software.

Essential Nodes

Here are the nodes you'll encounter most often:

NodePurpose
MediaInBrings your timeline clip into the Fusion composition. This is always the starting point — it represents the footage on your Edit page timeline.
MediaOutSends the final result back to the Edit page timeline. Whatever is connected to MediaOut is what appears in your edited video.
BackgroundGenerates a solid color, gradient, or transparent background. Useful as a base layer for motion graphics.
MergeCombines two inputs — a foreground and a background. This is how you layer elements together (similar to putting one clip above another on the timeline).
Text+The same Text+ you use on the Edit page, but with access to Fusion's full animation and effects system. Much more powerful in this context.
TransformMoves, scales, or rotates the image. Connect it between nodes to reposition elements.
Mask (Rectangle, Ellipse, Polygon, B-Spline)Creates a shape that can be used to mask (show/hide) parts of an image.

The Basic Flow: MediaIn → Merge → MediaOut

Every Fusion composition follows this fundamental pattern:

  1. MediaIn brings in your footage.
  2. A Merge node combines the footage with another element (like text or a background).
  3. MediaOut sends the result back to the timeline.

When you first open the Fusion page for a clip, you'll see MediaIn1 connected directly to MediaOut1. To add something on top of the footage, insert a Merge node between them, then connect your new element (text, image, shape) to the Merge's foreground input.

Creating a Simple Composition

Let's create a text overlay on your footage:

  1. On the Edit page, place the playhead on a clip and switch to the Fusion page.
  2. You'll see MediaIn1 → MediaOut1.
  3. Add a Text+ node: press Shift+Space to open the tool search, type "Text," and select Text+.
  4. Add a Merge node: press Shift+Space, type "Merge," and select it.
  5. Connect the nodes: drag from MediaIn1's output to the background input (yellow triangle) of Merge1. Drag from Text1's output to the foreground input (green triangle) of Merge1. Finally, connect Merge1's output to MediaOut1's input.
  6. Select Text1 and type your text in the Inspector. Adjust font, size, and position.
  7. Press 2 on MediaOut1 to preview the final result in the right viewer.
Tip: You can also connect nodes by dragging one node directly onto the line between two connected nodes. Fusion will automatically insert it into the flow and create a Merge node if needed.

Tracking: Planar Tracker

Tracking allows you to attach elements to moving objects in your footage. The Planar Tracker node analyzes a flat surface (like a sign, screen, or wall) in your video and calculates its motion over time. You can then lock text, images, or effects to that surface so they move naturally with the footage.

To use the Planar Tracker:

  1. Add a Planar Tracker node after MediaIn.
  2. In the Inspector, draw a shape around the surface you want to track.
  3. Click Track (forward and/or reverse). Fusion analyzes the motion.
  4. Set the operation mode (e.g., Corner Pin) and connect your overlay element.

Green Screen Keying: Delta Keyer

If you have footage shot on a green screen (or blue screen), you can remove the background color and replace it with anything:

  1. Add a Delta Keyer node after MediaIn.
  2. In the Inspector, use the eyedropper to click on the green area in the viewer. The green should become transparent.
  3. Refine the key using the Matte controls: Clean Black, Clean White, and edge softness.
  4. Use a Merge node to place the keyed footage over a new background.
Tip: Good keying starts with good footage. Even lighting on the green screen, no wrinkles, and the subject far enough from the backdrop all make a huge difference in how clean your key will be.

Key Takeaways

  • Fusion is DaVinci Resolve's built-in compositing engine, using a node-based workflow instead of layers.
  • The fundamental flow is MediaIn → Merge → MediaOut.
  • Key nodes: Background (solid color), Merge (combine elements), Text+ (text), Transform (move/scale/rotate).
  • Use the Planar Tracker to attach elements to moving surfaces and the Delta Keyer for green screen removal.
  • Nodes can feel unfamiliar at first, but they offer more power and flexibility than layer-based systems.
