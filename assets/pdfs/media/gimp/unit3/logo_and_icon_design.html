Logo & Icon Design

Designing Clean, Memorable Graphics in GIMP

Overview

Logos and icons are the visual identity of a brand, product, or project. Unlike photos or illustrations, they must be simple, recognizable, and effective at any size — from a tiny favicon in a browser tab to a large banner on a building. GIMP can be used to design effective logos and icons, especially when you leverage the Paths tool for clean shapes.

Design Principles

Simplicity

The best logos are simple. They use clean shapes, limited colors, and avoid unnecessary detail. Think of iconic logos like Apple, Nike, or Target — they are all easily recognizable because they are stripped down to their essential forms. A good test: can you draw your logo from memory?

Scalability

A logo must look good at all sizes. It should be clear and legible when shrunk to 32 pixels (a favicon) and still look sharp when enlarged to fill a poster. Avoid tiny details that disappear at small sizes, and avoid effects (like complex gradients or shadows) that break down when scaled.

Memorability

A good logo is distinctive and sticks in people's minds. Use a unique shape, an unexpected color combination, or a clever visual idea (like the hidden arrow in the FedEx logo). Avoid generic clip-art shapes and overused design clichés.

Appropriate Style

The logo's style should match its purpose. A children's toy brand might use round, colorful, playful shapes. A law firm would use clean, professional, conservative design. A tech startup might go for modern, geometric, minimalist styling.

Logo Design Process

Step 1: Sketch Ideas on Paper

Before touching the computer, sketch rough ideas with pencil and paper. Draw many quick variations — don't judge them yet. This brainstorming phase is where the best ideas emerge. Aim for at least 10–15 rough sketches before choosing a direction.

Tip: Sketching on paper is faster than designing on the computer and frees you from worrying about tools and technicalities. Focus on shapes and concepts.

Step 2: Create in GIMP Using Paths

Once you've chosen your strongest sketch, recreate it digitally in GIMP:

  1. Create a new image with a transparent background (File > New, fill with transparency).
  2. Use the Paths tool (B) to draw clean, smooth shapes with Bézier curves.
  3. Convert paths to selections (Select > From Path) and fill them with solid colors using the Bucket Fill tool.
  4. Use separate layers for each shape element so you can adjust independently.

The Paths tool gives you much smoother edges than drawing with the Paintbrush. For logos, clean vector-style shapes are essential.

Step 3: Add Text

If your logo includes a company name or initials, use the Text tool (T):

  • Choose a font carefully — the font communicates personality. Sans-serif fonts feel modern and clean; serif fonts feel traditional and authoritative; display fonts feel creative and unique.
  • Adjust letter spacing (tracking) in Tool Options for a polished look.
  • Consider modifying a letter or two to make the wordmark unique (e.g., replacing an "O" with a custom shape).

Step 4: Apply Colors

Use color intentionally:

  • Start with a 2–3 color palette maximum. Too many colors create visual clutter.
  • Make sure the logo works in black and white as well as color — logos are often printed in single-color formats.
  • Consider the psychology of colors: blue = trust, red = energy, green = nature, yellow = optimism, purple = creativity.

Working with Transparency

Logos almost always need a transparent background so they can be placed on any surface without a visible box around them.

  • Ensure the canvas layer has an alpha channel (Layer > Transparency > Add Alpha Channel).
  • Delete or hide the background layer so the logo sits on transparency (shown as a checkerboard).
  • Export as PNG — this format preserves transparency. Never use JPEG for logos, as JPEG doesn't support transparency and uses lossy compression that creates artifacts around sharp edges.
Warning: If you export as JPEG, your transparent background will turn white (or whatever background you had). Always use PNG for logos and icons.

Icon Sizes

Icons need to be created at specific sizes depending on where they will be used:

Use Case Recommended Size Notes
Favicon (browser tab icon) 32 × 32 px Must be extremely simple. Export as .ico or .png.
App icon 512 × 512 px Design at this size and scale down as needed.
Social media profile picture 500 × 500 px Will often be displayed in a circle, so keep content away from corners.
Tip: Design your icon at the largest size first (512 px), then scale it down using Image > Scale Image to test how it looks at smaller sizes. Simplify any details that become unclear when small.

Exporting

  1. Save the XCF project file with all layers intact.
  2. Export as PNG at full size for web use (with transparency).
  3. For icons, export at each required size as separate files (e.g., logo-512.png, logo-32.png).
  4. Optionally export a version with a white or colored background for use cases that require it.

Summary

  • Good logos are simple, scalable, memorable, and appropriate for their brand.
  • Sketch first on paper, then create digitally using the Paths tool for clean shapes.
  • Use 2–3 colors and ensure the design works in black and white.
  • Always use a transparent background and export as PNG.
  • Design icons at the largest size, then scale down and test at all needed sizes.
