2D Digital Art — GIMP

What Is GIMP?

An Introduction to the GNU Image Manipulation Program

Overview

GIMP stands for GNU Image Manipulation Program. It is a free, open-source image editing application that provides professional-grade tools for photo retouching, image composition, graphic design, and digital painting. Whether you need to crop a photo, create complex artwork, or design graphics for a website, GIMP has the tools to get it done — all without costing a penny.

Why GIMP?

The most popular image editor in the world is Adobe Photoshop, but Photoshop requires an ongoing subscription that can cost $20 or more per month. GIMP delivers the same core capabilities entirely for free:

  • Photo retouching and editing — color correction, cropping, exposure adjustments, blemish removal
  • Graphic design — posters, flyers, social media graphics, logos
  • Digital painting — brushes, tablet support, custom dynamics
  • Web graphics — optimized exports for PNG, JPEG, GIF, and WebP
  • Compositing — combining multiple images with layers, masks, and blend modes

If you learn GIMP, you will find that the skills transfer directly to Photoshop and other image editors. The menus, tool concepts, and workflows are remarkably similar.

Tip: Many professional designers use GIMP alongside or instead of Photoshop. Because it's open-source, you can use it at home, at school, or at work with no licensing concerns.

A Brief History

GIMP was first created in 1996 by Spencer Kimball and Peter Mattis as a project at the University of California, Berkeley. It was one of the first major free software applications and quickly gained a community of developers and users around the world.

Over the decades, GIMP has evolved significantly. Major milestones include the move to the GTK toolkit (which GIMP itself inspired), support for high-color-depth images, and a constantly expanding filter and plugin ecosystem. Most recently, GIMP 3.0 was released with a modernized interface, improved support for high-DPI displays, non-destructive editing features, better Wayland and macOS support, and enhanced painting tools with on-canvas controls.

What Can You Do With GIMP?

Photo Editing

GIMP includes a full suite of tools for adjusting brightness, contrast, color balance, levels, and curves. You can remove red-eye, fix old or damaged photos, and apply sharpening or blur effects. These are the same operations used by professional photographers worldwide.

Graphic Design

You can create posters, banners, business cards, social media images, and more. GIMP supports text tools, vector paths, gradients, patterns, and a huge library of filters and effects to bring your designs to life.

Digital Painting

GIMP supports pressure-sensitive drawing tablets and offers customizable brushes with dynamics for size, opacity, and color. You can paint directly on the canvas like you would on paper, build up layers of color, and create original artwork from scratch.

Web Graphics

Need a website header, a favicon, or optimized images for the web? GIMP lets you export images in all common web formats with fine-tuned compression settings to balance quality and file size.

Platform Availability

GIMP runs on all major operating systems:

  • Windows — download the installer from gimp.org
  • macOS — native build available from gimp.org
  • Linux — available through most distribution package managers or as a Flatpak

No matter what computer you use, GIMP works the same way. Your project files (XCF format) are fully cross-platform, so you can start a project on one OS and finish it on another.

The Price: Free Forever

GIMP is released under the GNU General Public License (GPL), which means it is free to download, use, share, and modify — forever. There are no trial periods, no watermarks, no feature-locked tiers, and no subscriptions. This makes it an ideal choice for students, hobbyists, educators, and professionals who want powerful image editing without ongoing costs.

Tip: You can download GIMP at https://www.gimp.org/downloads/. Always download from the official site to ensure you get a safe, unmodified version.

Summary

  • GIMP is a free, open-source image editor comparable to Adobe Photoshop.
  • It has been actively developed since 1996 and continues to receive major updates.
  • Use cases include photo editing, graphic design, digital painting, and web graphics.
  • It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
  • It will always be free — no subscriptions, no trial periods.
