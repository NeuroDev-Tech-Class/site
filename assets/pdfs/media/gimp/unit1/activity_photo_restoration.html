Activity: Photo Restoration

Bringing an Old or Damaged Photo Back to Life

Overview

In this activity, you will take an old, damaged, or faded photograph and restore it using the color correction and retouching tools you've learned. By the end, you'll have a clean, corrected image and experience with a real-world image editing workflow.

What You'll Need

  • An old or damaged photo. You can scan a family photo, download a public-domain vintage image, or use a practice image provided by your instructor. Look for photos with common damage: scratches, fading, discoloration (yellow or blue color casts), dust spots, or torn areas.
  • GIMP installed and open.

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1: Open and Duplicate

Open your photo in GIMP (File > Open). Immediately duplicate the background layer (Layer > Duplicate Layer) so you always have the original to compare against. Rename the duplicate something like "Restoration" by double-clicking its name in the Layers dialog.

Tip: Save the project as an XCF file right away (Ctrl+S) so you don't lose your work.

Step 2: Crop to Remove Borders

If the scanned photo has white borders, ragged edges, or excess background, use the Crop tool (Shift+C) to trim the image to just the photo itself. Click and drag to define the crop area, then press Enter to apply.

Step 3: Fix Contrast and Brightness with Levels

Go to Colors > Levels. Look at the histogram:

  1. Drag the black-point slider (left) to where the histogram data begins — this deepens the shadows.
  2. Drag the white-point slider (right) to where the data ends — this brightens the highlights.
  3. Adjust the midtone slider (center) left or right to fine-tune overall brightness.

Click OK when the image looks more balanced.

Step 4: Remove Color Casts with Color Balance

Old photos often have a yellow, sepia, or blue color cast. Go to Colors > Color Balance:

  • If the photo looks too yellow, push the Yellow–Blue slider toward Blue in the Midtones range.
  • If the photo looks too red or warm, push the Cyan–Red slider toward Cyan.
  • Adjust Shadows and Highlights separately if needed.

The goal is to make the colors look natural. If the original photo is black and white, you can skip this step or use Colors > Desaturate to ensure it's truly neutral gray.

Step 5: Repair Scratches and Damage

This is where the real restoration happens. Use these tools to fix physical damage:

  • Clone Stamp (C) — Ctrl+click on a clean area near the damage to set the source, then paint over the damaged area. The clean pixels are copied to replace the damage.
  • Healing Tool (H) — works the same way as Clone Stamp but blends the result with the surrounding area. Preferred for skin and smooth surfaces because it creates seamless repairs.

Work at a zoomed-in level for precision. Use a small brush size for fine details and a larger brush for broad areas. Take your time — this step requires patience.

Tip: Frequently change your source point (Ctrl+click) to avoid creating visible repeating patterns. Sample from areas that match the texture and brightness of the damaged spot.

Step 6: Sharpen the Image

Old scanned photos are often slightly soft or blurry. Apply gentle sharpening to bring back detail:

  1. Go to Filters > Enhance > Unsharp Mask.
  2. Start with these settings: Amount: 0.3–0.5, Radius: 2–3 px, Threshold: 0.
  3. Check the preview and adjust as needed. Don't over-sharpen — the image should look crisper, not grainy.
Warning: Over-sharpening creates ugly halos and noise. If you've gone too far, undo (Ctrl+Z) and try again with lower settings.

Step 7: Export Your Finished Image

Once you're happy with the restoration:

  1. Save the XCF project file (Ctrl+S).
  2. Export as PNG (Ctrl+Shift+E) for highest quality.
  3. Export as JPEG (use quality 85–95) for a smaller file suitable for sharing or printing.

Activity Checklist

  • ☐ Opened an old or damaged photo in GIMP
  • ☐ Duplicated the original layer before editing
  • ☐ Cropped to remove unnecessary borders
  • ☐ Used Levels to fix contrast and brightness
  • ☐ Used Color Balance to remove color casts
  • ☐ Used Clone Stamp and/or Healing Tool to repair damage
  • ☐ Applied Unsharp Mask to sharpen the image slightly
  • ☐ Saved the XCF project file
  • ☐ Exported as both PNG and JPEG

Reflection

After completing the restoration, compare your finished version with the original side by side (toggle the original layer's visibility). Consider: What damage was hardest to fix? Which tool was most useful? What would you do differently next time?
