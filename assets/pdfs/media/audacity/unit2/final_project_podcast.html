Audio Production — Audacity

Final Project: Mini Podcast Episode

Combine everything you've learned into a polished podcast-style audio piece

Overview

For this final project, you'll create a complete 2- to 5-minute podcast-style audio episode. This project brings together every skill from the course: recording, editing, noise cleanup, effects, multi-track mixing, and the Envelope tool. You'll deliver a finished, professional-sounding audio file that you can be proud of.

Content Requirements

Your podcast episode must include all of the following:

1. Introduction with Music Bed

  • Begin with background music (a "music bed") that plays for a few seconds.
  • Over the music, introduce your podcast: the title, your name, and a brief description of what the episode is about.
  • The music should duck (lower in volume) when you start speaking and fade out or remain low as you transition into the main content.

2. At Least Two Segments or Topics

  • Your episode should cover at least two distinct topics or segments (e.g., a "topic of the day" and a "tip of the week," or two different subjects you want to discuss).
  • Each segment should be clearly separated by a transition so the listener knows you're moving to a new topic.

3. Background Music

  • Use royalty-free music as background music, intro music, or transition music.
  • Music must be properly licensed for use. Use sources like:
    • Free Music Archive (freemusicarchive.org)
    • Incompetech (incompetech.com) — music by Kevin MacLeod
    • YouTube Audio Library (studio.youtube.com — Audio Library section)
    • Pixabay Music (pixabay.com/music)
    • Bensound (bensound.com)
  • You must list the music sources you used in a written companion document (see Submission below).

4. At Least One Sound Effect

  • Include at least one sound effect — a transition swoosh, a ding, applause, a notification sound, or anything appropriate.
  • Royalty-free sound effects can be found at:
    • Freesound (freesound.org)
    • Pixabay Sound Effects (pixabay.com/sound-effects)
    • Zapsplat (zapsplat.com)

5. Transitions Between Segments

  • Use at least one transition method between your segments:
    • Cross-fade between clips.
    • A short music bridge (a few seconds of music between segments).
    • A sound effect marking the transition.
    • A fade out / fade in combination.

6. Outro with Closing Remarks

  • End with a brief closing: thank the listener, recap what was covered, and sign off.
  • Bring the music back up (or introduce outro music) and fade out to end the episode cleanly.

Suggested Workflow

  1. Plan your episode. Decide on a topic, outline your segments, and write a loose script or bullet points for each segment.
  2. Find your music and sound effects. Download royalty-free music and at least one sound effect. Note the source and license for each.
  3. Record your voice. Set up your mic, test levels, and record your narration. Don't worry about mistakes — you'll edit them out.
  4. Edit your voice track. Remove mistakes, ums, long pauses. Apply Noise Reduction, then Normalize or Compressor.
  5. Import music and sound effects. Add them as separate tracks in your project.
  6. Arrange and position clips. Use the Time Shift tool (or clip dragging) to place everything at the right time.
  7. Set volume levels. Use the Gain slider on each track to balance your mix. Voice should be the loudest element.
  8. Apply envelope ducking. Use the Envelope tool on the music track to lower it under narration.
  9. Add transitions. Cross-fades, music bridges, or sound effects between segments.
  10. Final listen. Play the entire episode from start to finish. Check for: balanced volume, clean edits, natural-sounding transitions, and overall flow.
  11. Export. File > Export Audio > MP3, 192 kbps. Fill in metadata tags.
  12. Save your project. File > Save Project for future editing.
Warning: Start early. Recording, editing, and mixing takes more time than you expect — especially the first time. Don't wait until the last minute.

Submission Checklist

Before You Submit, Confirm:

  • ☐ Episode is between 2 and 5 minutes long.
  • ☐ Includes an introduction with background music.
  • ☐ Contains at least 2 distinct segments or topics.
  • ☐ At least 1 sound effect is included.
  • ☐ Transitions are used between segments (cross-fades, music bridges, or sound effects).
  • ☐ Includes an outro with closing remarks and a music fade-out.
  • ☐ Project uses at least 3 tracks (voice, music, effects).
  • ☐ Noise Reduction has been applied to the voice track.
  • ☐ Normalize or Compressor has been applied to the voice track.
  • ☐ Envelope tool was used to duck music under narration.
  • ☐ Exported as MP3 at 192 kbps.
  • ☐ Metadata tags (Title, Artist, Genre) are filled in.
  • ☐ Written companion document listing all royalty-free music and sound effect sources is included.
  • ☐ Audacity project file (.aup3) is saved and submitted.
Tip: Listen to your finished episode on different devices — headphones, laptop speakers, phone — to make sure it sounds good everywhere. If the voice is hard to hear on phone speakers, your music might be too loud.
