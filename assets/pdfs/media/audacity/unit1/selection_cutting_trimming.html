Audio Production — Audacity

Selection, Cutting & Trimming

Learn to select, cut, trim, split, and clean up audio recordings

The Selection Tool

Almost every edit you make in Audacity starts with a selection. The Selection tool is active by default, or you can press F1 to activate it.

  1. Click anywhere on the waveform to place your cursor.
  2. Click and drag left or right to highlight a region of audio.
  3. The selected region appears as a highlighted block on the waveform.

Making Precise Selections

  • Extend a selection: Hold Shift and click to extend the selection to a new point.
  • Adjust edges: Hold Shift and press the Left or Right arrow keys to adjust the selection boundary one sample at a time.
  • Select entire track: Press Ctrl+A to select all audio in the project.
  • Type exact times: Use the Selection Toolbar at the bottom to enter precise start and end times.

Snap-To

If you need your selections to snap to specific time boundaries, go to View > Snap-To and choose a snapping option (e.g., nearest second, nearest beat). This is helpful for projects with regular timing. For most editing, leave Snap-To off for the most control.

Core Editing Commands

Once you've selected a region, use these commands to edit it:

CommandShortcutWhat It Does
CutCtrl+XRemoves the selected audio and places it on the clipboard. The remaining audio closes the gap.
CopyCtrl+CCopies the selection to the clipboard without removing it.
PasteCtrl+VInserts the clipboard contents at the cursor position, pushing existing audio to the right.
DeleteDelRemoves the selected audio. The remaining audio closes the gap (like Cut, but doesn't copy).
Silence AudioCtrl+LReplaces the selected audio with silence. Does not close the gap — the timing stays the same.
Trim AudioCtrl+TKeeps only the selected audio and removes everything else on that track.
Tip: Delete and Silence Audio are different in an important way. Delete closes the gap (shortening the track). Silence Audio keeps the gap (preserving timing). Choose the right one depending on whether you need to keep the timeline aligned.

Splitting and Joining Clips

Split — Ctrl+I

Splitting divides a clip into two separate clips at the cursor position (or at the edges of the selection). The audio stays in place, but now you have independent clips that can be moved, deleted, or edited separately.

  1. Place your cursor at the point where you want to split.
  2. Press Ctrl+I.
  3. You'll see a thin vertical line indicating the split point. The two halves are now separate clips.

Join Clips

To join clips back together:

  1. Select across both clips (drag from one into the other).
  2. Go to Edit > Clip Boundaries > Join.

The clips merge back into a single continuous clip.

Undo and Redo

Don't worry about making mistakes — Audacity has unlimited undo within a session.

  • Undo: Ctrl+Z — Reverses the last action.
  • Redo: Ctrl+Y — Re-applies an undone action.

You can also go to View > History to see a full list of every action you've taken and jump back to any point.

Warning: Undo history is lost when you close the project. If you want to keep the ability to undo, save your project before closing.

Workflow: Removing Mistakes from a Recording

Here's a practical step-by-step for cleaning up a voice recording by removing "ums," "ahs," long pauses, and verbal slip-ups:

  1. Play through the recording and take note of where mistakes occur. You can drop labels (Ctrl+B) at each mistake for easy reference.
  2. Zoom in (Ctrl+1) on the first mistake so you can clearly see the waveform.
  3. Use the Selection tool to highlight the mistake — include the silence before and after the unwanted word to keep the edit tight.
  4. Press C (Play Cut Preview) to hear what the audio sounds like with the selection removed. This lets you check the edit before committing.
  5. If it sounds good, press Del to delete the selection.
  6. If the edit sounds abrupt, undo (Ctrl+Z) and adjust the selection boundaries.
  7. Repeat for each mistake.
Tip: When removing long pauses, don't cut out all silence — leave a short natural gap (about 0.3–0.5 seconds) so the speech doesn't sound unnaturally rushed.

Practice Exercise

Activity: Edit a Recording

  1. Record yourself reading a paragraph, but deliberately include some "ums," pauses, and a sentence you intentionally mess up.
  2. Use the Selection tool and Delete to remove those mistakes.
  3. Use Trim Audio (Ctrl+T) to keep only the best take.
  4. Listen to the final result — it should sound smooth and natural.

Key Takeaways

  • Select audio by clicking and dragging with the Selection tool (F1).
  • Ctrl+X cuts, Del deletes, Ctrl+L silences, and Ctrl+T trims.
  • Ctrl+I splits a clip into independent pieces.
  • Use Play Cut Preview (C) to test edits before committing.
  • Ctrl+Z undoes any mistake — use it freely.
