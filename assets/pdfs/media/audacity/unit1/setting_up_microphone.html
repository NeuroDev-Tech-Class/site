Setting Up Your Microphone

Configuring audio input so Audacity records clear, properly leveled sound

Why Setup Matters

Before you record a single word, taking a few minutes to configure your microphone saves hours of frustration later. A badly configured input can result in recordings that are too quiet, distorted, or completely silent. This guide walks you through every step.

Selecting Your Input Device

Audacity needs to know which microphone to listen to. There are two ways to choose your input device:

Method 1: Audio Setup Toolbar

  1. Look for the Audio Setup button in the toolbar area (or the microphone dropdown near the meter toolbar).
  2. Click the dropdown next to the microphone icon.
  3. Select your microphone from the list (e.g., "USB Microphone," "Realtek Audio," or your audio interface name).

Method 2: Preferences

  1. Go to Edit > Preferences (or press Ctrl+P).
  2. Click Audio Settings (or Devices in older versions).
  3. Under Recording, set the Device dropdown to your microphone.
  4. Click OK.
Tip: If your microphone doesn't appear in the list, make sure it's plugged in, then go to Transport > Rescan Audio Devices to refresh the list.

Choosing Channels

In the same Recording section, you'll see a Channels option:

  • 1 (Mono) — Records a single channel. Best for voice recordings, podcasts, narration, and any single-microphone setup.
  • 2 (Stereo) — Records two channels (left and right). Use this only if your device actually provides two separate channels (e.g., a stereo microphone or a two-input audio interface).
Warning: If you select Stereo with a mono microphone, one channel will be silent (or a duplicate). This wastes space and can cause issues during editing. Stick with Mono for a single mic.

Setting Your Recording Level

Getting the right recording level is one of the most important steps. Too quiet and your audio will be noisy when you turn it up later; too loud and it will clip (distort).

Step-by-Step Level Check

  1. Click the Recording Meter (the bar with the microphone icon at the top of the screen) to activate monitoring — you can also click Start Monitoring from the meter's dropdown.
  2. Speak into your microphone at the volume you'll actually use during recording. Don't whisper or shout — use your natural speaking voice.
  3. Watch the meter as you speak:
    • The peaks should land between -12 dB and -6 dB. This is the sweet spot.
    • If peaks are barely moving (below -30 dB), your input is too quiet.
    • If the meter hits 0 dB or turns red, your input is too loud.
  4. Adjust the recording volume slider in Audacity's Mixer toolbar, or adjust the gain knob on your microphone or audio interface, until peaks consistently fall in the -12 to -6 dB range.
Tip: It's always better to record a little too quiet than too loud. You can easily boost a quiet recording later, but you cannot fix clipped audio.

Test Recording Workflow

Before starting a real project, always make a quick test recording:

  1. Press R (or click the red Record button) to start recording.
  2. Speak a few sentences at your normal speaking voice and volume.
  3. Press Space (or click Stop) to stop recording.
  4. Press Home to move the cursor to the beginning.
  5. Press Space to play back your recording.
  6. Listen for: clear voice, no distortion, no background hum, reasonable volume.

If everything sounds good, delete the test track and start your real recording. If not, adjust your setup and test again.

Common Issues and Fixes

No Sound at All

  • Wrong device selected — Double-check that Audacity is set to the correct input device.
  • Microphone not connected — Make sure the mic is plugged in and recognized by your computer (check your system sound settings).
  • Microphone muted — Some mics have a physical mute button. Make sure it's off.
  • Permissions — On Windows 10/11, go to Settings > Privacy > Microphone and ensure microphone access is allowed for desktop apps.

Too Quiet

  • Increase the recording volume slider in Audacity.
  • Turn up the gain knob on your USB mic or audio interface.
  • Move closer to the microphone (6–12 inches is typical for speech).

Clipping / Distortion

  • Lower the recording volume slider or gain knob.
  • Move slightly farther from the microphone.
  • Avoid speaking directly into the mic; angle it slightly to the side to reduce plosives (hard "P" and "B" sounds).

Background Noise / Hum

  • Record in the quietest room available. Close windows and doors.
  • Turn off fans, air conditioners, and other noisy appliances.
  • Use a microphone with a cardioid pickup pattern — it rejects sound from behind and the sides.
  • We'll learn how to reduce noise in a later lesson using Effect > Noise Reduction.

USB Microphone Tips

  • Plug the mic in before opening Audacity so it's detected on startup.
  • USB mics have their own built-in sound card — select the mic itself as the input device, not your computer's default audio.
  • If your USB mic has a headphone jack, use it for monitoring to avoid latency (delay) issues.
  • Avoid using USB hubs — plug directly into your computer for the most reliable connection.
