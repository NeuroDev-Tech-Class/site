When most people picture an AI assistant, they imagine typing a question and reading a text answer. That’s still the heart of it — but tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude can now do a great deal more. Knowing what they’re capable of helps you reach for the right feature instead of fighting to describe everything in words. This reading is a quick tour of the most useful ones.

Multimodal AI: More Than Just Text

“Multimodal” simply means a model can work with more than one kind of information — not just text, but also images, audio, and files. In practice, that means you can:

  • Show it a picture. Upload a photo of a broken appliance, a math problem, a plant, or a screenshot of an error message, and ask the model what it sees or how to fix it.
  • Talk to it. Most assistant apps now have a voice mode, so you can have a spoken back-and-forth conversation instead of typing.
  • Hand it a document. Upload a PDF, slideshow, or spreadsheet and ask the model to summarize it, pull out key points, or answer questions about it.
  • Have it create images. The same assistant can often generate pictures too, so you don’t need a separate art tool for a quick image.

A picture is often worth a thousand words of prompt. If something is hard to describe — a layout, a diagram, a weird-looking bug — it’s usually faster to show the model than to explain it.

“Chat With Your Documents”

One of the most practical uses of AI for a student is feeding it material you’re already working with. Instead of asking the model to recall facts from memory (where it can hallucinate), you give it the actual source and ask questions about that source. For example:

Here are my lecture notes (attached). Quiz me on the five most important concepts, one question at a time, and tell me if I get them right.

Because the answer comes from the document you provided, it’s far more reliable than asking the model to remember your textbook on its own. This same idea — giving a model trusted source material to work from — is the foundation of many professional AI tools.

Custom Assistants

If you find yourself giving the model the same instructions over and over (“you are my study coach, explain things simply, quiz me at the end…”), you can save that setup once and reuse it. Each major tool has its own version of this:

  • ChatGPT — “Custom GPTs” and “Projects”
  • Gemini — “Gems”
  • Claude — “Projects”

A custom assistant is just a normal AI assistant with a saved role, saved instructions, and sometimes saved reference files. It’s the “system instruction” idea from the prompt-engineering reading, made permanent and reusable.

AI Agents: From Answering to Doing

So far everything we’ve described answers you. An AI agent goes a step further: it can take actions to accomplish a goal, often across several steps, with less hand-holding. Rather than just telling you how to do something, an agent can use tools — searching the web, running code, filling out a form, or using other apps — to actually get it done.

For example, instead of “list some flights to Chicago,” an agent might be asked to “find the cheapest non-stop flight to Chicago next Friday and put the options in a table,” then go search, compare, and report back on its own.

Agents are powerful but not magic. They can make mistakes mid-task and confidently head in the wrong direction. Always review what an agent did before trusting or acting on the result — especially anything involving money, accounts, or sending messages on your behalf.

Try It Out

Pick two of the features below and try each one with ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude:

  1. Image input: Take a photo of something around you (a label, a plant, a puzzle, a chart) and ask the AI to explain or analyze it.
  2. Chat with a document: Upload a PDF or your own notes and ask the AI to summarize it or quiz you on it.
  3. Voice mode: Have a short spoken conversation and notice how it differs from typing.
  4. Custom assistant: Create a simple Custom GPT, Gem, or Project with a saved role (for example, a tutor for one of your classes).
